# Measuring the Dependency Gap: Diagnosing Inter-Column Fidelity in Tabular Generative Models 

Jie Zhang stzhangjie@gmail.com Accenture Japan 

## Abstract 

Synthetic tabular data is valued for preserving not only each column’s marginal distribution but the dependencies between columns —structure that carries much of the discriminative signal for minority classes in imbalanced domains such as fraud and clinical risk. Yet the metrics most commonly used to certify synthetic tabular data are, we show, largely blind to inter-column dependency: a baseline that models every column independently (and therefore destroys all dependency) is judged indistinguishable from real data by the logistic-regression C2ST, and the pairwise Trend score is only partially sensitive. We introduce a dependency-aware fidelity diagnostic that decomposes a strong classifier two-sample test ( XGB-C2ST )into marginal, dependency, and numerical–categorical cross components, anchored between a worst-case fully-factorized reference (all dependency destroyed) and a best-case real-data oracle. Applying it to a state-of-the-art flow-matching generator ( TabbyFlow /EF-VFM), we find a real dependency gap that standard metrics miss; destroying dependency outright collapses minority-class utility, and the generator’s residual gap carries a smaller, consistent utility cost. We then ask whether this gap reflects a structural limitation of mean-field generative objectives. It does not: consistent with recent recovery results for variational flow matching, the objective is asymptotically exact. Yet the gap is stubborn—a 16 ×

increase in model capacity does not close it—pointing to the absence of direct dependency supervision rather than a capacity or structural limit. Consistent with this, and because the residual gap is higher-order, no cheap intervention closes it: not an in-model dependency mechanism, not post-hoc copula correction, and not the 16 × capacity increase—a caution for a field that assumes such fixes help. 

## 1 Introduction 

Generative models of tabular data are widely used for privacy-preserving data sharing (Patki et al., 2016; Jordon et al., 2019) and for augmenting under-represented classes. In imbalanced domains—credit-card fraud, clinical risk, churn—the information that separates the minority class from the majority often lives not in any single feature but in the joint configuration of features: a young account age is unremarkable alone, a large transaction is unremarkable alone, yet their co-occurrence is the tell. A synthetic generator that reproduces every column’s marginal distribution but scrambles how columns move together will therefore produce “averagely realistic” rows whose minority-class structure has been washed out. Preserving inter-column dependency is thus central to the usefulness of synthetic tabular data, and especially to its value for the minority class (He & Garcia, 2009). Recent state-of-the-art tabular generators are based on diffusion (Ho et al., 2020; Song et al., 2021) and flow matching (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024) adapted to mixed-type tabular data (Guzmán et al., 2025; Shi et al., 2025; Mueller et al., 2026). A practical question follows immediately: how do we know a generator preserved dependency? The metrics that dominate tabular-synthesis evaluation—column-wise and pairwise “Trend”/“Shape” scores and a logistic-regression classifier two-sample test (C2ST)—are, we show, poorly suited to answer it. A degenerate baseline that fits each column’s marginal independently and samples them independently (destroying all dependency) attains a logistic-regression C2ST of 0.995 –1.000 —judged indistinguishable from real data, as faithful as a strong generator—while Trend degrades only mildly 1arXiv:2607.21636v1 [cs.LG] 20 Jul 2026 (0.69 –0.78 ). (A note on scales: LR-C2ST and Trend are quoted on the SDMetrics quality scale, where 

1.0 means indistinguishable/perfect; our XGB-C2ST below is a raw discriminator AUC, where 0.5 means indistinguishable and 1.0 fully separable.) These metrics certify marginals, not dependency. We make the dependency question measurable. We use a classifier two-sample test with a strong, interaction-sensitive learner (gradient-boosted trees, XGB-C2ST ) and decompose its score into a marginal component, a dependency component, and a numerical–categorical cross component, each anchored between a fully-factorized lower bound ( FF , marginals only) and a real-data oracle upper bound. The decomposition turns “is dependency preserved?” into numbers a practitioner can read off. Using this diagnostic we report three findings. (i) Standard metrics are blind to dependency, while XGB-C2ST exposes a real dependency gap in a state-of-the-art flow-matching generator ( TabbyFlow /EF-VFM, Guzmán et al., 2025); the axis matters for the minority class—destroying dependency outright collapses minority-class F1, and the generator’s residual gap carries a smaller but consistent shortfall against the oracle. (ii) The gap is not a structural blind spot of the mean-field generative objective. Consistent with recent exact-recovery results for variational flow matching (Eijkelboom et al., 2026), the objective supervises only per-dimension posterior means, yet the deterministic flow it induces recovers the joint distribution in the infinite-capacity limit. But the gap is also not closed by a large ( 16 ×) increase in capacity, indicating an optimization/supervision bottleneck rather than a capacity—or structural—limit. (iii) Consistent with (ii), no cheap intervention closes the gap. The residual gap is higher-order (the generator already matches second-order cross-moments), so a covariance-matching fix has nothing to correct; adding an explicit in-model dependency mechanism does not help, and neither does post-hoc copula correction (Scassola et al., 2026) nor brute-force scale—an honest negative result cautioning that neither a dependency-specific architecture nor brute-force scale is the lever.

## 2 Background and Related Work 

Tabular generative models. Modern tabular synthesizers include GAN-based (Xu et al., 2019; Zhao et al., 2021), diffusion-based (Zhang et al., 2024; Shi et al., 2025; Kotelnikov et al., 2023; Kim et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2023), flow-matching-based (Guzmán et al., 2025; Mueller et al., 2026), and language-model-based (Borisov et al., 2023; Liu et al., 2023) approaches; for a broader survey, see Borisov et al. (2024). We center our study on TabbyFlow , the tabular instantiation of Exponential-Family Variational Flow Matching (EF-VFM) (Guzmán et al., 2025), a strong and representative flow-matching baseline. EF-VFM uses a mean-field 

variational posterior that factorizes over columns and a moment-matching objective; numerical columns are modeled with a multivariate-Gaussian head whose covariance is isotropic, and categorical columns with per-column categorical heads. This factorized structure is exactly what makes the question of preserved dependency salient, and motivates our focus. 

Evaluating synthetic tabular data. The dominant fidelity metrics are column-wise and pairwise “Shape”/“Trend” similarities (e.g. SDMetrics (Patki et al., 2016)) and a classifier two-sample test (C2ST) 2(Lopez-Paz & Oquab, 2017), which casts “are two samples from the same distribution?” as a discriminator’s accuracy. Recent work questions these metrics, showing that weak discriminators and pairwise statistics can saturate even for poor generators (Scassola et al., 2026; Platzer & Reutterer, 2021). We build on this critique constructively: rather than only showing the metrics fail, we provide a decomposition that localizes 

what they miss (dependency) and how much , using a strong tree-based discriminator that is sensitive to high-order interactions. 

Modeling dependency. Copulas separate marginals from dependence (Nelsen, 2006; Aas et al., 2009), and recent work embeds copula or dependency structure inside generative models. These efforts are, however, outside our setting: Carrasco-Pollo et al. (2026) introduce a structured (non-mean-field) variational flow matching but for simulation-based inference, and Huk & Damoulas (2026) model in-model copulas within flow/diffusion but for purely continuous scientific data. Neither targets mixed-type, class-imbalanced tabular generation. Our work is diagnostic rather than a new generator: we measure where dependency is lost and test whether such in-model mechanisms help. 

Mean-field and recovery. A natural hypothesis is that a mean-field (column-factorized) objective cannot 

represent inter-column dependency. Recent recovery results for variational flow matching show this hypoth-esis is false in the idealized limit: because the interpolation is affine in the (one-hot-encoded) endpoint, the mean-field optimum recovers the true per-dimension posterior means, the marginal velocity field is exact, and the induced deterministic flow transports to the true joint distribution—continuous and categorical alike (Eijkelboom et al., 2026). This result is the backbone of our analysis in §5: any measured dependency gap must be attributed to finite capacity, optimization, or discretization, not to a structural blind spot. 

## 3 A Dependency-Aware Fidelity Diagnostic 

Setup. Let real data x ∼ P have d columns partitioned into numerical and categorical blocks, x =(xnum , x cat ), and let Q be a generator’s distribution. We assess fidelity on a held-out real test set (never used for fitting any reference), and we report minority-class F1 and recall of a downstream classifier trained on synthetic data and evaluated on real test data as the utility axis. 

Strong classifier two-sample test. For samples SP , S Q we form a balanced two-sample set (down-sampling to equal size, so chance accuracy is 0.5), label real as 1 and synthetic as 0, and train a gradient-boosted-tree discriminator (Chen & Guestrin, 2016) with 5-fold stratified cross-validation, reporting the out-of-fold AUC canonicalized as c2st = max(AUC , 1 − AUC) . Read as: 0.5 means indistinguishable (good), 

1.0 means trivially separable (bad). We use gradient-boosted trees rather than a linear model precisely because dependency is carried by high-order feature interactions that a linear discriminator cannot see; this choice is what makes the dependency component visible (Lopez-Paz & Oquab, 2017; Scassola et al., 2026; Grinsztajn et al., 2022). 

Decomposition. We separate marginal from dependency fidelity by destroying dependency while preserv-ing marginals. Let Π( ·) independently permute the rows of each column (the same procedure applied to each set, with independent per-column permutations), so Π( S) retains every column’s marginal but removes all inter-column dependence. Define 

full = c2st( SP , S Q), marg = c2st(Π( SP ), Π( SQ)) , dep = full − marg .

joint structure. To isolate the mixed-type interaction we further define a cross component via block permu-tation: pairing row i’s numerical block xnum

Reference points. We anchor the dependency axis with two references built from the real data. The fully-factorized reference FF resamples each column independently from its empirical marginal: it has correct 3marginals and zero dependency, providing a lower bound on dependency fidelity (its dependency gap is essentially the entire gap). The oracle is a fresh subsample of real training data—correct marginals and 

correct dependency—providing an upper bound ( XGB-C2ST ≈ 0.5). A generator’s dependency fidelity is read relative to these anchors. 

Protocol and discipline. All evaluations are deterministic (fixed seeds, fixed discriminator state). The real reference is always the held-out test set, never a set used to fit a reference, avoiding circularity. We report mean ±std over five seeds (three for the magic anchor references, as noted) and never select single runs. We use XGB-C2ST , the dependency components, minority-class F1/recall, and the FF /oracle bounds as the only fidelity criteria; we report Trend and logistic-regression C2ST only to demonstrate their blindness, never as criteria. 

## 4 What Standard Metrics Miss 

We apply the diagnostic to TabbyFlow /EF-VFM (Guzmán et al., 2025) on five class-imbalanced datasets (Dua & Graff, 2017): adult and default (categorical-rich schemas, adequately powered test sets—our strongest evidence), bank (Moro et al., 2014) (large, categorical-rich, imbalanced), magic (a numerical-only control), and shoppers (small test set, qualitative only). For each dataset we report the generator against the fully-factorized lower bound ( FF ) and the oracle upper bound over five seeds (Table 1). 

Standard metrics are blind to dependency. The zero-dependency FF reference—which destroys 

all inter-column structure by construction—is nonetheless scored as near-perfect by the logistic-regression C2ST on each of the four datasets in our metric-blindness panel ( default was not part of this panel):

Trend is partially sensitive (it scores FF at 0.69 –0.78 rather than 1.0), so we rest the blindness claim on the LR-C2ST. A practitioner relying on these metrics would conclude that destroying every dependency costs almost nothing. XGB-C2ST , by contrast, correctly separates FF from real at 0.956 –0.995 (Figure 2): the metrics in common use certify marginals, not joint structure. 

A real dependency gap, consistent across datasets. ## 5 Is the Gap a Structural Blind Spot? No 

The dependency gap in Section 4 admits three competing explanations. (H1) The mean-field objective is structurally unable to recover inter-column dependency—its optimum loses it. (H2) It is recoverable, but the baseline lacks the capacity to do so. (H3) It can and has ample capacity, but the objective provides no direct signal that would close the gap. We rule out H1 (a recovery theorem) and H2 (scaling the model 16 ×

does not help), and argue for H3: the deficit lies in what the objective rewards, not in what the architecture 5can represent or how large it is. The distinction is practical—it predicts that neither an explicit dependency module nor brute-force scaling closes the gap, which is what we observe (Section 6).

There is therefore no structural blind spot, and any observed gap must be attributed to finite capacity, optimization, or sampling discretization rather than to the objective’s form. The decoding step is not the culprit (controlled XOR). A remaining worry is specific to categor-ical generation: EF-VFM decodes each categorical column independently at the final step, which could in principle discard joint categorical structure. We test this with a controlled task in which a third categorical variable is the exclusive-or of two others ( C3 = C1 ⊕ C2), so all pairwise correlations are zero but the three-way joint is fully determined—precisely the kind of higher-order dependence a second-moment view would miss. An EF-VFM-style model trained on this controlled task recovers the joint—measured as the fraction of generated rows satisfying C3 = C1 ⊕ C2 (real data: 1.0)—at 0.98 with five sampling steps and 1.00 with twenty, despite independent per-column decoding. Independent decoding thus does not lose joint categorical structure once the velocity field is accurate, consistent with the recovery argument above.

The insufficient-capacity account (H2) predicts that scaling the model up should shrink the dependency component. ## 6 Can In-Model Fixes Close It? 

The H3 account of Section 5 predicts that an explicit dependency mechanism—an architectural change, not a change to what the objective supervises—should not close the gap. We test this prediction directly, and report an honest negative. 

An explicit in-model dependency mechanism. Guided by the column-type attribution of the gap (on adult the dependency is dominated by the numerical–categorical cross component), we augment the generator with a discrete-aware cross-coupling head that lets categorical predictions condition on a learned summary of the other columns, breaking the per-column independence of the baseline’s categorical head. We verify, via a sampler-level check, that the added mechanism actually enters the generated samples (and is not merely a training-loss term). We then compare the modified model against the unmodified baseline using a paired protocol: for each of five matched seeds we form per-seed differences ∆i = fix i − base i and report mean ±std and sign counts, which removes shared initialization and sampling noise. We pre-set the acceptance bar: a significant decrease in the dependency component, a significant increase in minority F1,and a genuine decrease in full XGB-C2ST .The intervention produces no significant change on any of the three datasets we test. On adult the targeted component changes by ∆dep -cross = +0 .002 ± 0.003 (the wrong direction); on bank by −0.006 ± 0.006 ;on default by +0 .003 ± 0.009 —in every case the standard deviation is at least the magnitude of the mean, i.e. no significant change. Minority-class F1 likewise shows no significant improvement anywhere (∆F1 = +0 .009 / + 0 .002 / + 0 .005 , all within noise), and the full XGB-C2ST shows no significant change (on 

bank the point estimates move in the favorable direction—all five seeds’ full XGB-C2ST decrease slightly, 

p ≈ 0.09 —but remain within noise). None of this meets the pre-set bar. The sampler-level check passed in all three runs, so this is a failure of the remedy , not of the mechanism’s wiring. We stress that the paired protocol only removes shared noise—training trajectories still diverge—and sign counts are screening, not significance; we therefore read the magnitudes, which are within noise. A single-seed preview on adult had looked encouraging and reversed sign once all five seeds were in, a cautionary reminder against single-run claims. 

The residual gap is higher-order; post-hoc and resampling routes do not supply it. Three observations sharpen why no fix helps. First, the residual gap is higher-order : the generator already matches the second-order numerical–categorical cross-moments of the real data to near the oracle floor (a mean standardized moment gap of 0.067 /0.042 /0.053 on adult /default /bank , versus 0.052 /0.033 /0.051 for the oracle and 0.248 /0.196 /0.173 for the zero-dependency FF ; five seeds). A second-moment or covariance-matching fix therefore has essentially nothing to correct—the mismatch lives in higher-order structure such a term does not see (hence we do not test one). Second, injecting dependence post hoc into the generator’s marginals via fitted copulas (Gaussian copula, vine copulas (Aas et al., 2009), and empirical variants, tuned on validation) at best matches the baseline on XGB-C2ST —the empirical variant, in a prior controlled study under the same protocol—while worsening higher-order statistics and downstream utility; indeed a Gaussian copula fails to fit these mixed strong-dependency datasets at all (its XGB-C2ST sits at the ≈ 1.0 ceiling), so it cannot even supply second-order structure, let alone higher-order. Third, a resampling baseline (SMOTE (Chawla et al., 2002)) reaches minority-class F1 comparable to training on real data (with higher recall), but by reusing real majority rows and linearly interpolating minority samples rather than synthesizing the joint; its fidelity is dominated by the real component, so it neither exhibits the generator’s gap nor speaks to whether a generator can recover dependence—it shows only that good minority utility is reachable by real-data oversampling, a fact orthogonal to the generative-dependency question. 

Takeaway. No cheap intervention closes the gap. An in-model dependency module does not (above); the residual gap is higher-order, so a covariance-matching fix has nothing to correct; post-hoc copula correction cannot supply the missing structure; and—per Section 5—scaling capacity 16 × leaves the gap essentially 7untouched. This is consistent with dependency being unsupervised by the objective: there is no efficient lever. The gap is guaranteed to vanish only in the asymptotic capacity limit; supervising dependence directly in the objective is, on our evidence, the remaining candidate lever—a useful, if unglamorous, guide for practice, and a caution against assuming that an added module or more scale will help. 

## 7 Discussion and Limitations 

What we claim, and how strongly. The qualitative findings—metric blindness, the dependency ↔utility link, and the absence of a structural blind spot—hold across datasets and, for the structural claim, follow from the recovery argument and the XOR control. The quantitative dependency gap is significant on all four quantitative datasets (one-sample t, p ≤ 0.02 ), strongest on adult and default ; shoppers is qualitative only (small test set). Our capacity finding rests on width sweeps on adult , bank , and magic , where the larger models converge cleanly (paired t, all p > 0.05 ); on default , training does not optimize reliably at any width and its capacity cell is excluded rather than interpreted. Trend is partially sensitive to destroyed dependency (scoring the FF reference 0.69 –0.78 ), so the blindness claim rests on the LR-C2ST. 

Approximations. The dependency decomposition is additive in XGB-C2ST -AUC space; the resulting “share” should be read as a discriminability share, not as a fraction of a statistical divergence. The numerical– categorical cross component is isolated by a block permutation that destroys cross-coupling while preserving within-block structure. 

Guidance for practitioners and method designers. For practitioners evaluating tabular synthesizers: report the dependency decomposition with fully-factorized and oracle anchors, and do not rely on Trend or logistic-regression C2ST to certify dependency, since a zero-dependency baseline passes them. For method designers: in our experiments neither an added dependency module nor a 16 × capacity increase closed the gap; the binding constraint appears to be what the objective supervises, so progress likely requires objectives that reward dependence directly. Finally, an open question left by our analysis is to characterize which 

dependence structures are hard to recover and at what rate; we do not give such a characterization here.
