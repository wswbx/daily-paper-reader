Language Models

Abstract
Repeating Transformer layers can improve
language-model performance without adding
parameters. A pretrained model can be adapted
to support both single-pass and looped infer-
ence by reusing middle-layer weights for addi-
tional computation. Because both paths share
weights, training to improve looped predictions
can degrade single-pass quality. We study joint
training strategies using weighted losses on
both outputs, with guidance from the frozen
original model. Joint supervision substantially
reduces single-pass degradation compared to
looped-only training, while preserving looped
gains over ordinary continued training. Most
of this benefit comes from adjusting the single-
pass loss weight, with guidance from the frozen
original model further improving the looped
output. With validation-selected weights, our
method preserves single-pass quality within
a predefined tolerance of ordinary continued
training while improving looped predictions.

Introduction
Repeating Transformer layers gives a language
model additional computation without requiring
another set of layer weights. Recent work has
shown that pretrained models can be adapted to ben-
efit from such repeated computation (Koishekenov
et al., 2025; McLeish et al., 2025; Marchenko et al.,
2026). The adapted model retains its single-pass
path, which applies each layer once, alongside a
looped path that reuses the same weights to repeat
a subset of layers. The same model can then use
the single-pass path when computation is limited
and the looped path when additional computation
is available.
We study adaptation that preserves all pretrained
layers and the original output head. Because both
paths share all weights and remain trainable, updat-
ing the model to improve looped predictions also
changes the weights used by the single-pass path.
The training challenge is to preserve single-pass
quality while making the looped path useful.
Evaluating whether adaptation succeeds requires
comparing against two baselines:
the starting
checkpoint and the alternative of continuing to train
the original model without looping. We call the lat-
ter ordinary continued training. Keeping the start-
ing quality is not enough if the same budget would
improve the original model further.
A natural training approach is to supervise both
outputs with next-token labels. We also consider
adding a teacher signal: using one prediction’s
probability distribution to guide the other predic-
tion during training. This resembles distillation
in standard teacher-student setups, adapted here
to two paths that share all weights. We compare
weighted label losses on both outputs with and
without this teacher signal, starting either from the
initial checkpoint or after an initial period of train-
ing only the looped path. The staged setting allows
us to examine how single-pass quality can be recov-
ered while preserving useful looped predictions.
Adding a teacher signal introduces a different
supervision target for one output. An improvement
over training both paths with labels alone might
also be achievable by giving that output’s label
loss more weight. We include a stronger label-only
baseline to test this possibility, with its weight cho-
sen to account for the added teacher term (§3.2).
This comparison measures how much of the ob-
served benefit the teacher signal provides beyond
stronger label supervision.
Training cost also matters, because computing
the teacher signal takes time even when two meth-
ods execute the same number of model layers. We
therefore compare methods under separate budgets
based on layer evaluations and measured training
time, including any teacher computation.
The teacher signal can come from different
sources. One option uses the current looped output
as teacher for the single-pass output, recomput-

ing its distribution at each training step. Another
option uses a frozen copy of the original model,
which provides a fixed reference throughout adap-
tation. We compare these two sources and examine
whether the teacher signal primarily benefits the
output that receives it directly or also improves the
other output through their shared weights.
We make three main contributions:
• Joint supervision reduces single-pass degra-
dation and supports recovery. We find that
applying a small loss weight to the single-pass
output substantially reduces the degradation
caused by training only the looped path. Dur-
ing recovery, supervising both outputs pre-
serves more looped quality than returning to
single-pass training alone, although it recov-
ers less single-pass quality.
• Increasing the single-pass loss weight re-
produces most of the benefit from adding
a teacher signal to guide the single-pass
output.
Without a teacher signal, adjust-
ing the loss weight on the single-pass output
alone recovers most of the observed improve-
ment. After validation-based weight selection,
adding the teacher signal shows no additional
mean improvement over the selected label-
only baseline at matched training time.
• A frozen teacher from the original model
can preserve single-pass quality while
improving the looped output.
We call
this method Frozen-original KD. With a
validation-selected label weight, it uses the
original model’s predictions to guide the
single-pass output. In the main setting, this
method preserves single-pass quality within
a predefined tolerance of ordinary continued
training while improving looped predictions
over that baseline.
The results separate two training choices: the
loss weight on each output and the source of
the teacher signal. With validation-selected label
weights, Frozen-original KD preserves single-pass
quality within the predefined tolerance of ordinary
continued training while improving looped predic-
tions over that baseline in the main setting. This
joint result does not extend to the secondary Wiki-
Text corpus. Evaluating both outputs against ordi-
nary continued training is necessary to distinguish a
useful supervision choice from successful retention
of both prediction paths.

Related Work
Recurrent conversion.
Encode, Think, De-
code repeats pretrained middle layers while re-
taining the physical stack (Koishekenov et al.,
2025). Retrofitted Recurrence studies conversion
and training curricula under compute constraints
(McLeish et al., 2025), while Closing the Loop
uses input mixing and smoothed exit-loss weights
(Marchenko et al., 2026). Shapiro (2026) studies a
recurrent retrofit with an identity-preserving one-
loop path. These works adapt pretrained models to
benefit from looped computation, establishing the
feasibility of converting existing models without
retraining from scratch.
Supervision across depths.
RRT combines inter-
mediate label losses with detached deeper-output
distillation in compressed recursive models (Bae
et al., 2025). LoopFormer uses trajectory condi-
tioning and stop-gradient consistency across com-
putation budgets (Jeddi et al., 2026). Concurrent
work studies frozen and dynamic cross-loop teach-
ers through on-policy distillation for mathematical
reasoning, using student-generated rollouts and re-
verse KL (Wang et al., 2026). These works su-
pervise models at multiple computation depths.
Our setting differs in that we retain the complete
single-pass path alongside the looped path, with
both paths sharing all weights, and use fixed-text
next-token supervision with forward KL.
Distillation and retention controls.
Guidance
from an existing predictor and soft-target regular-
ization have prior precedents (Li and Hoiem, 2016;
Yuan et al., 2020). Distillation and label smooth-
ing need not have identical effects (Sultan, 2023).
We compare single-pass label strength and teacher
source for two paths that share all weights. Both
the single-pass and looped outputs are evaluated
against ordinary continued training, with teacher
computation included. The coefficient-matched la-
bel control tests how much of the improvement
from adding a teacher signal can be reproduced
by stronger label supervision alone. It does not
identify the remaining difference with a particular
mechanism.

Training Strategies
3.1
Two Predictions from Shared Weights
The decoder has eight Llama-style blocks (Touvron
et al., 2023). Let E contain the embedding and
first two blocks, M the middle four blocks, D the
2


last two blocks, and H the final normalization and
vocabulary head. The prediction paths are
fs(x; θ) = H(D(M(E(x)))),
fc(x; θ) = H(D(M(M(E(x))))).
(1)
The single pass applies all eight blocks once. The
looped path applies twelve blocks. At the starting
weights θ0, fs is exactly the original model. Both
paths share all parameters, and all weights remain
trainable. Joint computation shares the first six
block applications before branching, so one joint
update uses fourteen forward-block applications.
Appendix A gives the architecture and implemen-
tation.
3.2
Labels, Distillation, and a Matched
Control
Let Ls and Lc be the single-pass and looped cross-
entropies, averaged over valid targets. The Joint
CE baseline and Online KD objective are
LCE = 0.1Ls + 0.9Lc,
(2)
LKD = LCE + 0.1 KL(sg[pc]∥ps).
(3)
Online KD uses the current model’s looped output
as its teacher. Its distribution is recomputed at
each update and detached in the KL term. The
looped label loss remains fully differentiable. Both
distributions use temperature 1, and KL covers the
full vocabulary, averaged over valid targets. This
uses the current looped output to guide the single-
pass output, distinguishing it from guidance by a
frozen predictor at the initial checkpoint.
Our coefficient-matched label control, Matched
CE, uses
Lmatched = 0.2Ls + 0.9Lc.
(4)
The coefficients are not normalized by 1.1. For
one target with label vector ey, the single-pass
logit gradients of KD and matched CE are 0.2ps −
0.1ey−0.1pc and 0.2ps−0.2ey. Matching the coef-
ficient on ps controls one change introduced by KL.
It does not match gradient direction, norm, clip-
ping, or parameter updates. This tested coefficient-
matched control is one label-weight setting, not an
optimized label-supervision frontier. Appendix E
examines those differences.
Ordinary and loop-only training optimize Ls and
Lc separately. A lightly weighted joint baseline
uses 0.05Ls + 0.95Lc. Joint CE, KD, and matched
CE start either at the ordinary parent or after 2,048
loop-only updates. The staged ordinary control
instead follows the same switch state with ordinary-
only updates. The ten methods and their update
counts appear in Table 1.
3.3
Our Method: Frozen-original KD
Our method, Frozen-original KD, guides the cur-
rent single pass using a frozen copy of the original
single-pass predictor. We retain next-token label
losses on both current outputs and use
L(q; α) = αLs + 0.9Lc + 0.1 KL(sg[q]∥ps). (5)
For our method, q = ps(θ0). The frozen teacher
performs an independent forward pass; all student
weights remain shared and trainable. Teacher com-
putation is included in the training budgets, and the
teacher is not used at inference. Teacher-source
controls replace q with the current looped predic-
tion pc(θt) or the frozen initial looped prediction
pc(θ0), keeping both label weights and the KL coef-
ficient unchanged. The initial teacher-source study
uses α = 0.1. In the label-weight study, valida-
tion selects α = 0.3 for Frozen-original KD; the
teacher and KL coefficient remain unchanged.
Additional controls. Target-content controls
flatten or permute non-label probabilities. Both
preserve the gold-token probability. Permutation
also preserves entropy and the probability multi-
set, changing their assignment to vocabulary to-
kens. Early-only guidance uses online KD for 512
updates, then matched CE; delayed guidance re-
verses this order. Both label objectives remain ac-
tive throughout. Appendix G gives all seven added
conditions and the fixed permutations.
For label-weight selection, we compare
LCE,m = mLs + 0.9Lc,
LKD,m(q) = (m −0.1)Ls + 0.9Lc
+ 0.1 KL(sg[q]∥ps),
(6)
with m ∈{0.2, 0.4, 0.8, 1.6}. Here m is the nomi-
nal coefficient on the single-pass distribution in its
logit gradient, not always its label-loss coefficient.
Coefficients are not normalized. Equal m does not
imply equal full gradients, clipping, or parameter
updates.

Experimental Setup
4.1
Models and Data
The main experiments adapt two fixed 133,830,912-
parameter models, C and D, after 32,768 ordinary
3


updates. In the initial supervision and target studies,
each model has three training-data orders, giving
six paired units per method. Within a unit, meth-
ods restore the same weights, optimizer buffers,
random state, and data position. Orders permute
the same windows within predetermined segments.
The window sets agree at every registered segment
boundary. The six units are repetitions of adapta-
tion within two parents, not six independent pre-
training runs. Historical continuations are excluded
from these summaries.
Training uses a two-billion-token pool of En-
glish FineWeb-Edu text (Penedo et al., 2024), a
GPT-2 tokenizer, sequence length 1,024, and batch
size 32. A fresh validation set contains 524,288
targets in sixteen blocks. A separate confirmation
set contains 2,097,152 targets in thirty-two blocks.
Exact document hashes exclude recorded training
and earlier evaluation text. Previously inspected
WikiText (Merity et al., 2017) supplies 282,624
targets in eighteen clusters as a secondary corpus.
The supervision study uses this confirmation set
for all primary NLL comparisons. Appendix A.1
reports counts and data terms.
A separate target study adds seven conditions in
the same six parent–order units, giving 42 new end-
points. Fresh validation and confirmation text have
the same target counts as above but different doc-
ument sets. Existing controls are re-evaluated on
the new text. These interventions reuse the two par-
ents and six orders; they add no pretraining seeds.
The two studies retain separate result tables. The
subsequent label-weight study uses new validation
and confirmation documents with the same target
counts; Appendix H distinguishes the three studies.
4.2
Training Methods and Schedules
Names prefixed by Loop→apply the named
objective after 2,048 loop-only updates.
Thus
Loop→KD uses joint labels and online distillation
during recovery. Figures call the schedules From
start and After loop-only; appendix tables call them
static and staged. The separate 512-update guid-
ance controls retain both label objectives from the
start.
The label-weight study compares the three strate-
gies over the same four-point grid. Selection uses
validation results from C1 and D1 at the fixed
training-time endpoint. Within each family, both
single-pass gaps must meet their retention margins
on each development order. We then select the
admissible weight with the lowest mean looped val-
idation NLL and evaluate looped utility after selec-
tion. The chosen weight is fixed before evaluation
on C2, C3, D2, and D3, using new confirmation
text and paired fixed-time ordinary endpoints. Ap-
pendix H gives the thresholds, tie-breaking rule,
and handling of infeasible families.
4.3
Training and Evaluation Budgets
Muon and auxiliary AdamW retain the inherited
learning rates 0.004 and 0.00006 throughout adap-
tation and stage switches (Jordan, 2024; Loshchilov
and Hutter, 2019). The initial studies use six RTX
5090 GPUs with 32 GB each; the label-weight
study and additional configurations use eight. Each
training process uses one GPU.
The forward-block cap is 40,960 applications
at fixed batch size. Ordinary, loop-only, and joint
updates use 8, 12, and 14 applications. A staged
joint path uses 2, 048×12+1, 170×14 = 40, 956
applications. An ordinary suffix uses 2, 048 × 12 +
2, 048 × 8 = 40, 960. Different schedules there-
fore receive different update counts and training
exposure.
The second cap is 2,227.587 seconds of synchro-
nized forward computation, loss, backward com-
putation, gradient clipping, and optimizer work.
Loading, evaluation, and checkpoint I/O are ex-
cluded. Staged time includes the newly trained
loop-only prefix. Every trajectory covers the cap,
and its time endpoint is the last saved update within
the cap. The two endpoints come from the same
training trajectory. GPU assignments rotate across
paired units and method rounds. Appendix F sepa-
rates method timing from total experiment costs.
Target interventions are first compared at 2,925
student updates. Equal updates fix student expo-
sure but do not equate teacher cost. Frozen looped
and single-pass teachers add 12 and 8 block appli-
cations per update, giving totals of 26 and 22. At
the 40,960-block cap, their endpoints are 1,575 and
1,861 updates. Time comparisons include teacher
computation and reused prefixes.

5.1
Joint Supervision Retains More
Single-Pass Quality
Table 1 and Figure 1 show all ten methods at
the forward-block cap.
Loop-only training im-
proves looped NLL over ordinary continued train-
ing by 0.009048, but worsens single-pass NLL by
0.171239. Giving the single-pass label loss 5%
weight reduces that gap to 0.035882 while retain-

looped prediction over ordinary continued training

across all six paired units. The ordinary suffix re-
stores more single-pass quality, while joint recov-
ery retains more looped quality.

5.3
The Residual Depends on the Resource
Budget
In the isolated benchmark, KD takes 20.9% more
update time than Joint CE and 15.3% more than
Matched CE, despite equal forward-block counts.
A balanced follow-up removes the CE–Matched
CE timing gap under a common weighted-CE im-
plementation but reproduces it with the historical
implementations; the difference is implementation-
dependent (Appendix F). Comparisons retain the
actual trajectories’ complete-update times.
At the time cap, KD’s mean single-pass differ-
ences from Matched CE are −0.001025 from the
start and −0.001009 after loop-only training. Ad-
justed text intervals are below zero, while condi-
tional order intervals span zero. Looped differences
are −0.000006 and −0.000456, with text intervals
spanning zero. The residual is unresolved across
continuation orders at this budget; the results do
not establish equivalence. Time-matched static KD
instead has higher single-pass NLL than Matched
CE on WikiText. Appendix B reports both corpora,
all effects, and separate uncertainty intervals.

5.5
Label-Weight Selection and Joint
Retention
The initial fixed-coefficient studies do not identify
a configuration meeting all four criteria on their
primary confirmation texts (Appendices C and G).
We now compare the recipes selected under the
common rule in §4. Label-only CE and Online KD
select m = 0.8, while Frozen-original KD selects
m = 0.4.
On the four held-out continuation orders, se-
lected Frozen-original KD meets all four point-
estimate criteria in each order. Family-adjusted
text intervals for the four-order mean also sup-
port all four criteria (Table 2; Appendix H). The
single-pass interval relative to ordinary continua-
tion crosses zero, supporting retention within the
predefined margin rather than superiority. Unlike
selected label-only CE, the frozen-teacher recipe
retains useful looped predictions (Figure 2).
Relative to selected CE, its looped NLL im-
proves by 0.0119, with a conditional data-order
interval of [−0.0202, −0.0036]. The separately ad-
justed text interval supports the same direction. Se-
lected Online KD shows no additional mean im-
provement over selected CE. These comparisons
concern the tested weight grid, not a globally opti-
mal label-only objective.
5.6
Receiving Path and Additional
Configurations
With the same frozen teacher and total KL weight,
guidance to the single pass, looped output, or both
improves mean looped NLL relative to its respec-
tive label control. Direct looped guidance also
improves the single pass relative to its own con-
trol. The effect is therefore not unique to single-
pass guidance. The controls use the original low
single-pass label weight and do not identify the
best receiving path for the selected recipe.
At the original fixed coefficients, the frozen
teacher also improves looped NLL over the label
control in a deeper 8 →16 configuration and na-
tive Parcae. Neither extension meets the full joint
criteria. These are fixed-recipe extensions, not tests
of the selected label weight from the main setting.
Appendix H reports their separate configurations
and results.

Predictions

continuation by 3.53 percentage points for the sin-
gle pass and 2.83 for the looped output. Paired
item intervals support both reductions, whereas
conditional training-order intervals span zero. Hel-
laSwag accuracy remains lower on average than
ordinary continuation. These are task-specific re-
tention gains for the fitted models, not stable im-
provement across orders or tasks (Appendix H).
6
Analysis
Label strength and teacher source.
Increasing
the single-pass label weight narrows the gap from
ordinary continuation but can remove the looped
advantage. The full grid shows that Frozen-original
KD balances the two outputs at an intermediate
weight (Appendix H). Guidance from the original
model follows the use of old-model outputs during
continued learning (Li and Hoiem, 2016); its value
here depends on its combination with label super-
vision. The receiving-path controls show that the
looped benefit is not unique to guiding the single
pass. They do not rank the receiving paths for the
selected recipe because their label controls differ
and their weights were not jointly selected.
Additional computation is not uniformly benefi-
cial.
Extra latent iterations can turn correct pre-
dictions into errors (Fu et al., 2025); our diagnostic
also contains both corrections and new errors (Ap-
pendix E). The selected recipe meets the primary
text criteria but both outputs have higher NLL than
ordinary continuation on WikiText. This does not
isolate repeated computation as the cause or iden-
tify a noise-amplification mechanism. The gain
over selected label-only training is measured at the
same looped inference depth. Whether using the
looped rather than single-pass output justifies its
extra inference cost is a separate deployment ques-
tion, not established by training-time comparisons.
Retention depends on the reference.
Ordinary
continuation measures adaptation’s opportunity
cost. New fixed-time and initial validation-selected
references involve different recipes, texts, and av-
eraging units; subtracting their gaps is not a con-
trolled improvement. Retention within 0.005 is
distinct from equal NLL or superiority. Updates
can change which examples are correct even when
accuracy improves (Xie et al., 2021), motivating
separate loss and correctness-retention measures.
7
Conclusion
Adapting a pretrained model for repeated computa-
tion requires evaluating both its original and looped
predictions. Stronger single-pass labels reproduce
most of the improvement from adding a teacher
signal in the initial comparisons. With validation-
selected weights, Frozen-original KD retains single-
pass NLL within the predefined margin of ordinary
continued training and improves looped NLL over
that reference in the main setting. Its looped output
also improves over selected label-only training at
matched training time. The results support compar-
ing label strength and teacher source jointly. The
corpus and task results delimit this conclusion to the
evaluated text-retention setting.
8


Limitations
The main experiments study two fixed 133.8M-
parameter pretrained models on English text, with
additional tests of recurrence depth and a native
recurrent architecture. The comparisons use a fi-
nite set of label weights and teacher configurations
under specified training budgets, with uncertainty
reported separately for evaluation data and contin-
uation orders. Experiments at larger model scales,
in other languages, and under generation-time bud-
gets are natural directions for further study.
Ethical Considerations
The study uses existing English corpora and pub-
lic benchmarks without new human participants
or annotation. Web text can contain biased, harm-
ful, copyrighted, or personally identifying material.
Likelihood evaluation and exact deduplication do
not remove those risks. We conducted no sepa-
rate personal-information or harmful-content audit.
Appendix A.1 describes data and software terms.
Appendix F reports the available compute records.
Energy use and emissions were not measured.

D.1
Evaluation and Per-Task Comparisons
We evaluate both paths on ARC-Easy, PIQA, and
HellaSwag (Clark et al., 2018; Bisk et al., 2020;
Zellers et al., 2019). Evaluation uses 2,376 test,
1,838 validation, and 10,042 validation items, re-
spectively, with zero-shot official harness templates
and uncached full-option likelihood. ARC-Easy
and PIQA use accuracy; HellaSwag uses the har-
ness length-normalized accuracy. Final task splits
never select checkpoints or hyperparameters.
Mean accuracy can hide losses on previously
correct examples. For each parent, let S0 be the
examples answered correctly by its original single
pass. Let Fm(S0) be the fraction of S0 missed by
method m. We report the excess loss of correct
predictions
Em = Fm(S0) −Fordinary(S0).
(9)
Both fractions use the same S0 and the same bud-
get’s ordinary reference. Negative Em favors the
candidate. Appendix D gives all methods, both
budgets, and item-level uncertainty.
The task results are mixed (Table 13). Staged
KD’s single pass improves ARC-Easy accuracy
over ordinary continuation by 1.333 percentage
points (pp), with E = −1.704 pp. The same output
changes PIQA by −0.091 pp and HellaSwag by
−0.037 pp. Its looped output changes the three
task accuracies by +1.340, −0.299, and −0.053
pp. The ARC-Easy mean improvement is therefore
not a uniform task gain.
Loop-only training further separates average ac-
curacy from retention. Its single pass changes Hel-
laSwag accuracy by +0.106 pp, yet loses 1.941 pp
more of the parent’s correct examples than ordi-
nary continuation. Parent HellaSwag accuracies

are 26.9% and 27.4%, with a 25% uniform-choice
reference. The six-run means are descriptive, and
the task comparisons retain their individual accu-
racy and retention measures.
Model updates can introduce errors despite
higher mean accuracy (Xie et al., 2021). Think-at-
Hard studies correct predictions revised into errors
by additional latent iterations (Fu et al., 2025). Our
fixed-path comparisons instead measure adaptation-
induced losses against ordinary continued training.
The common original-correct set sizes for C/D
are 1,043/1,024 on ARC-Easy, 1,062/1,081 on
PIQA, and 2,699/2,756 on HellaSwag. They are

E.1
What the Label Control Matches
For a valid target y, temperature 1, and single-pass
logits zs, the single-path contribution of KD is
∇zsLKD = 0.1(ps −ey) + 0.1(ps −pc)
= 0.2ps −0.1ey −0.1pc.
(10)
Matched CE contributes 0.2ps −0.2ey. Both re-
tain 0.9Lc. The computation matches a nominal
coefficient before clipping and optimizer transfor-
mations. Because the paths share weights, full
parameter gradients also include the looped label
term and contributions through the shared prefix.
The algebra is not an equality of full updates.
The local diagnostic restores fourteen fixed
states, comprising two original parents, six loop-
only states after 2,048 updates, and six joint CE
suffix states after 256 suffix updates. CE, matched
CE, and KD each receive one preview update on
the same fixed engineering batch, for 42 updates.
The fixed batch contains 32 windows and is not a
selected training or evaluation batch.
Across the fourteen states, mean clipping coeffi-
cients are 0.831672 for CE, 0.799955 for KD, and
0.741951 for matched CE. The independently re-
constructed parameter-update L2 norms for Muon
group 0 are 0.871143, 0.871155, and 0.871159,
respectively. Similar update magnitudes in one pa-
rameter group do not establish equal directions or
trajectories. The diagnostic covers 126 parameter-
group updates.
E.2
Scope of the Update Diagnostics
Muon can modify gradient buffers during the op-
timizer step. Norms measured afterward therefore
describe the modified buffers, not the gradients
immediately after clipping. The parameter-update
norms above are computed from parameter differ-
ences before and after the preview update. Com-
ponent gradient angles are diagnostic observations
and are not used as causal evidence.
E.3
Agreement and Prediction Quality
Static forward KL is 0.036555 for CE, 0.026262
for KD, and 0.026378 for matched CE (Table 15).
Staged forward KL is 0.045731 for CE, 0.035060
for KD, and 0.035759 for matched CE. KD fixes
1.452% of tokens and breaks 1.266%; matched
CE fixes 1.453% and breaks 1.270%. The close
means are descriptive and do not establish equiv-
alence. The KD single-pass NLL is 3.636666 on
this diagnostic subset, compared with 3.625906 for
ordinary continuation. Improved agreement does
not remove the ordinary reference gap.
The staged objectives retain a larger looped ad-
vantage within the same model than the static ob-
jectives. Their differing histories, label coefficients,
and optimizer states prevent assigning the differ-
ence solely to teacher maturity.
