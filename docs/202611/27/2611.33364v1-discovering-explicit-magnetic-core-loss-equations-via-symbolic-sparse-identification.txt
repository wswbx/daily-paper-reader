Abstract —Compact core loss equations are essential tools in magnetic component design, but existing approaches trade accuracy against interpretability. Empirical models such as the Steinmetz Equation (SE) yield compact expressions of limited accuracy, while machine learning methods achieve high accuracy as black-box predictors. This paper bridges the gap with aSymbolic Sparse Identification (SSI) framework, which casts core loss modeling as a data-driven symbolic regression problem and recovers unknown equations directly from measured data, without presuming a functional form. First, a comprehensively expanded library of symbolic candidate terms far richer than the SE is constructed. Second, the governing terms are isolated from this library by sparse regression. Third, the coefficients of the retained terms are refined with an AdamW optimizer to maximize accuracy and form an analytical equation. Experiments on Fair-Rite 78, 79, and 95 yield test mean absolute percentage errors of 1.89%, 1.01%, and 1.56%, respectively, with only 10, 10, and 7 active terms. Comparisons with empirical, numerical, and symbolic models quantify the resulting balance of accuracy and compactness. SSI thus provides an explicit and physically guided approach for high-frequency magnetic characterization and design. 

Index Terms —Core loss, equation discovery, high-frequency magnetics, sparse identification, symbolic regression. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

The need for higher power density in power electronics de-mands increasing operating frequencies, making magnetic core loss an important efficiency and thermal constraint [1]–[10]. It is therefore essential to have accurate loss prediction for material selection and magnetic design. An explicit equation as a predictor is exceptionally useful because it presents scaling relationships and can be directly integrated into optimization. For more than a century, magnetic core loss has been characterized by the Steinmetz Equation (SE) and its variants [11], [12]. Under sinusoidal excitation at a fixed temperature, the SE expresses volumetric core loss as Pv = kf αBβ ,where f is the operating frequency and B is the peak flux

density. However, it also has the limitation that a three-parameter power law cannot precisely represent the nonlinear behavior of magnetic materials in a wide operating range, leading to structural errors and poor accuracy. Modified and generalized Steinmetz equations extend core-loss prediction to nonsinusoidal excitation [13], [14], but prescribed analytical structures are retained and structural errors might still exist. Recent machine learning (ML) approaches have largely improved the accuracy by capturing nonlinear magnetic char-acteristics from measured data, at the expense of relinquishing the explicit equation rather than refining it [15]. Random forest [16] and feedforward neural networks [17] reproduce measured core loss with errors of a few percent by absorbing the loss nonlinearities into a learned mapping. However, they yield a numerical predictor rather than an analytical expression, and improved accuracy does not restore the scaling information that the SE conveys explicitly, which is insufficient for practical magnetic design. ML-based symbolic regression addresses this issue by iden-tifying analytical expressions directly from data. Specifically, the state-of-the-art Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dy-namics (SINDy) selects functions from a predefined library [18], whose principle can also be applied to the static loss surface. However, conventional polynomial sparse regression with arbitrary coefficient signs cannot represent nonnegative amplitudes of physical loss mechanisms. Following the path, this paper proposes a Symbolic Sparse Identification (SSI) framework to solve the core-loss equation discovery problem while maintaining physical interpretabil-ity. Specifically, SSI formulates the core-loss modeling as a symbolic sparse regression problem and constructs a fixed-grid physics-guided candidate library based on magnetic-loss behaviors. Dominant candidates are isolated by a hybrid objec-tive, AdamW updates, and a pruning mechanism. A compact equation with nonnegative coefficients is ultimately identified from measurements. II. S YMBOLIC SPARSE IDENTIFICATION FRAMEWORK 

The proposed SSI framework is shown in Fig. 1. Specifi-cally, a fixed symbolic library is first evaluated using normal-ized experimental data and its candidate coefficients are then optimized. Weak candidates will be pruned and the retained coefficients are refined to obtain an explicit core-loss equation. The details are introduced below. 

A. Problem Formulation of Core-Loss Modeling 

In macroscopic magnetic characterization, measured core loss can be regarded as an aggregate response with non-linear dependencies. Classical core-loss descriptions present hysteresis, eddy-current, and excess contributions, while high-frequency behavior introduces further curvature [19], [20]. D. Training Evolution 

The training histories in Fig. 4 show how the sparse equa-tions are identified. It can be seen that the objective decreases rapidly during the initial updates. When delayed pruning begins, the active term count falls in discrete steps, while the surviving coefficients adjust according to the reduced library. Ramped thresholding progressively reduces the representation from 103 initial functions to at most ten retained terms, and the retained amplitudes stabilize during fixed-support continuation. The 1200 warm-up updates are followed by 30,001 discovery updates, with pruning starting after the first 30% of discovery (approximately cumulative epoch 10,200). The saved runs then continue AdamW fitting on fixed support to cumulative epoch 60,000 before final refinement. Figure 4 displays only the first 40,000 cumulative updates, including the initial part of this continuation.

Therefore, SSI provides the best balance among sparsity, explicit equation, and physical interpretability. IV. P HYSICAL IMPLICATIONS AND LIMITATIONS 

The discovered equations retain the analytical value of empirical loss models while allowing several nonlinear depen-dencies to be selected from data. Their power-law exponents, roll-off scales, and logarithmic corrections describe how the measured loss changes with frequency and flux density. Local logarithmic sensitivities can therefore be evaluated directly from the identified expression. However, the retained functions only provide an effective macroscopic description rather than a unique microscopic loss separation. Several fixed-grid candidates are correlated, and more than one sparse combination may describe similar mea-sured behavior. Specifically, the fixed library determines the available resolution, so a scaling law between grid values may require several nearby terms. A denser grid increases accuracy and correlation simultaneously, thus reducing sparsity. Validation is limited to sinusoidal excitation at 25 ◦C within the measured envelopes. Temperature variation, DC bias, nonsinusoidal waveforms, and extrapolation require additional measurements and candidates. V. C ONCLUSION 

This paper proposed a Symbolic Sparse Identification framework that discovers explicit magnetic core-loss equa-tions from measured data. SSI casts core loss modeling as a data-driven symbolic regression problem without presuming a functional form, and constructs a comprehensively expanded physics-guided library of 103 symbolic candidate terms rep-resenting different loss dependencies. The dominant candi-dates are isolated from the library by a hybrid logarithmic-percentage objective with nonnegative AdamW updates and threshold pruning, and their coefficients are optimized to maximize accuracy. Experiments produce explicit equations with 10, 10, and 7 terms for FR78, FR79, and FR95, achiev-ing test MAPEs of 1.89%, 1.01%, and 1.56%, respectively. The results present improved accuracy over the SE while maintaining analytical transparency. Comparisons with RF, FNN, and SINDy quantify the superior balance of SSI among accuracy, compactness, and physical interpretability. SSI thus provides a feasible approach to discover explicit equations for magnetic characterization and design. Future work will extend to broader temperature and excitation conditions.
