# Scaling Looped MoE Language Models under Matched Budgets: Information Flow and Parameter Allocation 

Shipeng Bai †, Jiayu Qin †, Hong Liu †, Xili Wang †, Jianchao Tan *, Jiacheng Li He Zhang, Yifan Lu, Jiaqi Zhang, Kefeng Zhang, Yuchen Xie, Xunliang Cai 

Meituan 

Abstract 

Looped Transformers decouple computational depth from parameter count, and prior stud-ies have demonstrated promising performance gains with increased computation. However, systematic design principles for configuring these models under strict iso-parameter and iso-FLOP constraints remain scarce. We con-tribute to this direction by improving informa-tion flow in looped models and systematically studying loop parameter configurations. Specif-ically, we refine this information flow via gated inner-loop state updates and a cross-stage resid-ual connection. Building on this design, con-trolled studies identify a configuration combin-ing a single-pass Core depth of 25%–35% of the total unrolled depth with reallocating saved parameters into extra routed experts per layer, improving performance at zero extra compute while preserving compatibility with existing training infrastructure. Evaluated after 1T pre-training tokens, our 69B MLA and 51B KDA Mixture-of-Experts models outperform budget-matched non-recurrent baselines by an average of 1.16 and 2.29 percentage points across four benchmarks, respectively. Further reproduc-tion experiments on a common backbone with a 200B-token training budget show that our method achieves lower perplexity than other looped methods. Code for these reproduction experiments is available at GitHub.

Although prior studies have demonstrated the promise of looped architectures, they offer lim-ited guidance on how to scale them systemati-cally under matched parameter and compute bud-gets. Reusing weights across iterations requires balancing input preservation against progressive state updates, whereas varying the recurrent span and iteration count shifts the trade-off between ef-fective computational depth and unique parame-ter capacity—inherently coupling loop topology with budget allocation. These structural dependen-cies motivate two central research questions: In-formation Flow within the Loop: Under weight sharing, how should inner-loop state updates and cross-stage representation transfer be structured to enable effective iterative refinement? Existing looped models predominantly organize recurrent computation into a three-stage Prelude– Core–Coda topology (Geiping et al., 2025; Prairie et al., 2026), iteratively updating hidden states by combining them with input representations. To address the first question, we refine internal in-formation flow through inner-loop state updates 

and a cross-stage residual connection . Firstly, for inner-loop state updates, we stabilize feature scales across loop iterations via boundary normal-ization and combine historical states with injected inputs through independently controlled gated fu-sion. Secondly, we add a cross-stage residual con-nection linking the Prelude directly to the Coda to restore the direct residual pathway and gradient flow interrupted by boundary normalization and gated fusion. Together, these simple yet effective designs establish a stable recurrent flow that rec-onciles low-level feature fidelity with iterative se-mantic evolution, yielding substantial performance gains. Beyond information flow within the loop, sys-tematically scaling recurrent models requires prin-cipled loop configuration and cross-architecture generality. While recent studies have begun extend-ing looped architectures to larger-scale models— such as SMELT (Wang et al., 2026), which eval-uates looping the middle half of a sparse MoE twice under matched budgets, and SenseNova-Loop (OpenSenseNova, 2026), which integrates recurrence with hybrid attention and Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) (Xie et al., 2026)—existing efforts remain restricted to iso-lated topologies or fixed configurations, leaving generalizable allocation principles largely unex-plored. To bridge this gap, we systematically in-vestigate the joint design of loop topology and parameter capacity under strictly matched bud-gets, i.e., iso-parameter and iso-FLOP constraints. Our controlled studies reveal that effective scal-ing hinges on a structural trade-off between shared computational depth and independent parameter capacity : core partitioning and capacity restora-tion must be jointly determined. Guided by this insight, we formulate two transferable design prin-ciples: (1) Recurrent Core Configuration: Al-locating 25% –35% of the total unrolled depth to the single-pass depth of the shared Core balances computation reuse against independent boundary-layer capacity; and (2) Parameter-to-Expert Re-allocation: Transferring weight-sharing parameter savings into extra routed experts per layer improves performance at zero compute cost, offering a bet-ter quality–compatibility trade-off than alternative restoration strategies that alter layer or module di-mensions. To evaluate architectural generality, we apply this design to Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) mod-els equipped with Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) (DeepSeek-AI, 2024) and Kimi Delta At-tention (KDA) (Kimi Team, 2025). After 1T pre-training tokens, the 69B MLA and 51B KDA looped models outperform budget-matched non-recurrent baselines across four downstream bench-marks, delivering average gains of +1.16 and +2.29 percentage points, respectively. Furthermore, on a common Qwen3-style MoE backbone (Qwen Team, 2025) trained for 200B tokens, our method achieves lower perplexity than both Parcae and SMELT. 3.2 Inner-Loop State Updates 

Within the weight-shared Core, iterative refinement requires coordinating the fixed input representation with evolving hidden states across passes. We for-mulate inner-loop state updates through a direct first-iteration pass, boundary normalization, and a channel-wise gated fusion operator M (Figure 2).

3.3 Cross-Stage Residual Connection 

In a standard Pre-Norm Transformer without loop boundaries, an unobstructed direct residual path-way connects early representations to later layers. In the recurrent architecture above, however, insert-ing NE before the first Core pass and boundary normalization Nt−1 (together with gated fusion 

M) between iterations—while essential for align-ing feature scales and preventing growing recurrent states from overwhelming ˜e—interrupts this direct residual pathway from the Prelude to the Coda. Breaking this shortcut restricts direct gradient prop-agation back to the Prelude and forces the termi-nal state hR to simultaneously encode multi-step refinements and preserve initial low-level represen-tations. To restore a direct residual pathway without com-promising internal scale alignment, we introduce a 

cross-stage residual connection that bypasses NE ,the shared Core, and all boundary normalization layers Nt−1. Specifically, we add the unnormalized Prelude output e directly to the final recurrent state 

hR before entering the Coda: 

z = C(e + hR). (6) This single cross-stage addition introduces no ex-tra trainable parameters, preserves an unobstructed gradient path from the Coda to the Prelude, and allows the recurrent Core to focus on learning com-plementary iterative refinements on top of the ini-tial representation. 

4 Loop Configuration and Parameter Allocation 

Building upon the information-flow design in Sec-tion 3, we systematically examine the joint config-uration of loop topology and parameter capacity. At a fixed unrolled depth and per-layer configura-tion, changing the loop partition alters the number of independently parameterized layers and, con-sequently, the capacity that must be restored to meet the parameter budget. We therefore study loop configuration and budget restoration jointly: first investigating recurrence count and Core depth under iso-parameter and iso-FLOP budgets, then 4comparing restoration strategies and evaluating the effect of parameter restoration.

4.2 Parameter Budget Restoration Strategies 

To compensate for the independent parameter ca-pacity removed by weight sharing, existing ap-proaches adopt different budget-restoration strate-gies. SMELT (Wang et al., 2026) jointly adjusts model depth and width, narrowing the hidden di-mension to accommodate additional recurrent com-putation and increasing the expert count to restore parameter capacity. LoopMoE (Chen et al., 2026) instead expands the MLA Q/KV low-rank projec-tions, reduces individual expert widths, and en-larges the expert pool, redistributing capacity be-tween attention and FFN modules. We incorporate these restoration strategies into our recurrent framework and compare three vari-5ants on an MLA-MoE backbone: depth–width re-allocation (Depth–Width), attention–FFN realloca-tion (Attn–FFN), and uniform layer-wise expert-pool expansion (Ours). Our strategy restores the pa-rameter budget by increasing expert counts across physical layers while retaining the hidden dimen-sion, attention configuration, per-expert FFN width, and number of activated experts. All variants use the same training data, tokenizer, and learning-rate schedule, with iso-parameter and iso-FLOP budget targets, and are evaluated at approximately 100B training tokens.

5.1 Experimental Setup Models and budgets. To evaluate recurrent com-putation at scale, we consider two MoE language models whose attention modules are implemented with MLA and KDA, respectively. The MLA model has 4.37B active and 69B total parame-ters, including 31B over-embedding (OE) parame-ters (Liu et al., 2026); the KDA model has 3.24B active and 51B total parameters, including 10B OE parameters. Each recurrent model is paired with its corresponding non-recurrent baseline under iso-parameter and iso-FLOP budgets, and all main ex-periments use 1T training tokens. Total parameter counts include shared weights once, whereas active scales follow the unrolled execution convention, counting each access to the shared Core. Following the configuration principles in Sec-tion 4 ( R = 2 , ρ ∈ [25% , 35%] ), we configure the MLA and KDA models with unrolled depths 

Dtotal = 28 (ρ = 28 .6% ) and Dtotal = 32 

(ρ = 25 .0% ), respectively, using symmetric Pre-lude and Coda stages (full layer breakdowns in Appendix A). Both configurations preserve their backbone-specific attention computations while incorporating the same inner-loop state updates and cross-stage residual connection. Reallocating the parameters saved by weight sharing into ex-tra routed experts per layer restores the removed capacity within the prescribed budgets. 

Evaluation. We assess model performance on MMLU (Hendrycks et al., 2021), CMMLU (Li et al., 2024), C-Eval (Huang et al., 2023), and BBH (Suzgun et al., 2023). Recurrent models and their paired baselines share the same evaluation scope and scoring protocol. Scores are reported as percentages and gains as percentage points (pp). The aggregate score is the unweighted mean across all four evaluations, and the aggregate gain is com-puted relative to the corresponding non-recurrent baseline. 

Additional experiments. Comparisons with Par-cae and SMELT use a common GQA-MoE back-bone (Ainslie et al., 2023; Qwen Team, 2025) with approximately 0.8B active and 7.4B total param-eters, trained on 200B tokens from a fixed subset of the public Dolma 3 Mix dataset. Component ablations use an MLA-MoE recurrent model with approximately 1.3B active and 14.6B total param-eters, including 4.23B OE parameters, evaluated near 100B tokens. The mHC comparison uses the same token budget and approximately 1.3B active parameters in both models, with 14.5B total param-eters for the non-recurrent baseline and 11.9B for the recurrent model; both include 4.23B OE param-eters. Detailed training protocols, implementation choices, and parameter accounting are provided in Appendices A–C. 

5.2 Main Results 

The unified recurrent design yields consistent gains across both attention architectures under iso-parameter and iso-FLOP budgets. As shown in Table 2, the recurrent models outperform their non-recurrent counterparts on all four evaluations after 1T training tokens. The average score increases from 67.70 to 68.86 with MLA and from 65.42 to 67.71 with KDA, corresponding to improvements of 1.16 and 2.29 pp. The strongest improvements occur on BBH, where the MLA model advances from 48.09 to 50.95 and the KDA model from 44.24 to 48.32, yielding gains of 2.86 and 4.08 pp, respectively.

5.5 Integration with mHC 

Because our looped design explicitly structures inner-loop state updates and cross-stage residual connectivity, a natural concern is whether its gains overlap with recent architectures that enhance inter-layer residual information flow, such as Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) (Xie et al., 2026) and Attention Residuals (Kimi Team, 2026). 6 Conclusion 

We presented a framework for scaling looped MoE language models under matched budgets that jointly addresses information flow and parameter allocation. Inner-loop state updates with gated fu-sion stabilize iterative refinement, while a cross-stage residual connection preserves a direct resid-ual pathway from the Prelude to the Coda. Allo-cating 25% –35% of the unrolled depth to a two-pass shared Core and reallocating the parameters saved by weight sharing into extra routed experts per layer yields consistent gains over iso-parameter and iso-FLOP baselines with MLA and KDA back-bones up to 69B parameters, remains effective alongside mHC, and achieves lower perplexity than prior looped methods. Future work includes ex-tending the analysis to larger recurrence counts and token-adaptive depth, and measuring end-to-end inference efficiency. 

Limitations 

Our study focuses on pre-training looped MoE lan-guage models from scratch. First, we do not investi-gate warm-starting or retrofitting looped recurrence from existing non-recurrent checkpoints. Every Transformer block contains an MoE module, with no shared experts. C Ablation and mHC Experimental Details 

C.1 mHC Configuration 

The recurrent mHC model matches the unrolled depth of the non-recurrent mHC baseline using a Prelude–Core–Coda topology in which the shared Core executes twice ( R = 2 ), while retaining the original post-recurrent fusion and normalization boundaries in the Coda. Both models use four mHC residual streams, hid-den dimension 1536, and MLA attention with 16 heads. The query and key–value low-rank dimen-sions are 768 and 256, respectively, and the expert intermediate dimension is 512. The expert-pool configuration is unchanged between the baseline and recurrent model; the latter does not restore the capacity removed by sharing. Both have approx-imately 1.3B active parameters. Total parameter counts are approximately 14.5B and 11.9B, respec-tively, including approximately 4.23B OE parame-ters in each model. The comparison uses the same training-token budget. 

C.2 Component Ablation Configuration 

The ablations use MLA-MoE models without mHC. All recurrent variants share the same two-pass Prelude–Core–Coda layout ( R = 2 ), with hidden dimension 1536 and expert intermediate dimension 512. MLA uses 16 heads with query and key–value low-rank dimensions of 768 and 256. The recurrent variants incorporate uniform expert-pool restora-tion to match the parameter budget of the depth-matched non-recurrent reference (approximately 1.3B active and 14.5B total parameters, including 4.23B OE parameters), resulting in approximately 1.3B active and 14.6B total parameters (including 4.23B OE parameters). Across recurrent ablation variants, the backbone and restored expert capacity are held fixed. Remov-ing or replacing the fusion module changes only a small number of projection and control parameters. The full method and the variant without the cross-stage residual connection share the same inner-loop state update. Additive fusion replaces the gated op-erator while retaining direct first-iteration input, and the random-initialization concatenation variant applies concatenation-based linear fusion from a random initial state across both passes. Parameter scales are estimated from the effec-tive architecture configurations and the repository’s parameter-counting script. Total scales are reported to approximately 0.1B and OE scales to 0.01B. Ac-tive scales include the output head and exclude input embeddings and OE; they use the config-ured average expert-activation budget and count shared blocks at each unrolled visit. At approxi-mately 1.3B, the reported precision reflects small additional fusion and connection parameters and does not imply an exact count of distinct active weights for each routed token. 

C.3 Loss Aggregation 

The mHC and ablation experiments use sequence length 8,192 and global batch size 2,048, corre-sponding to 16,777,216 tokens per optimizer step. Training LM loss is averaged over the last 100 complete steps before 100B tokens: the inter-val [5861 , 5961) , covering approximately 98.33B– 99.99B tokens. Validation loss is the unweighted mean over our 20 held-out validation sets (span-ning general domains, knowledge, math, and code) at step 6,000, corresponding to approximately 100.66B tokens. We retrieve and freeze each complete metric se-ries before aggregation. If retries produce multiple records for the same step, the record with the latest timestamp is retained. All comparisons use these fixed snapshots and the same aggregation rules. Loss differences, when reported, are the variant’s loss minus the corresponding reference loss. 14
