# LoopCache: Accelerating Long-Context Self-Speculative Decoding for Looped Transformers 

Zizhuo Fu Haochen Huang Shengxuan Qiu Linye Wei Ziyu Tang Runsheng Wang Meng Li ∗

Peking University 

Abstract 

Looped Transformers achieve large effective depth with compact models by repeat-edly applying a weight-shared block across R recurrent steps, each maintaining a loop-specific key-value (KV) cache. To accelerate sequential loop execution, recent wave-style self-speculative methods like WaveFront Decoding (WFD) and LoopSpec pipeline draft and target tokens across loops in a single step. However, they face two critical limitations. First, they draft only at fixed loops and ignore intermediate prediction changes, wasting computation on stale trajectories. Sec-ond, batching tokens from different loops loads up to R× the KV cache per step, severely amplifying memory traffic in long-context scenarios. To address these challenges, we propose LoopCache, an efficient self-speculative decoding frame-work for long-context Looped Transformers. Based on the correlation between prediction switches and output confidence, LoopCache introduces confidence-guided dynamic draft updating to refine drafts in flight across all intermediate loops. Furthermore, LoopCache employs cyclic loop KV cache scheduling that rotates through loop-specific KV caches, restricting per-step KV traffic to a single loop while batching tokens at the nearest matching cycle for lossless verification. By also selectively retaining independent KV caches for a few loop-sensitive shallow layers, LoopCache improves acceptance rates with negligible overhead. Experi-ments show that LoopCache achieves average 10.25 × and 14.56 × speedup over WFD and LoopSpec. Code repository: https://github.com/zZzZ9zZ9/LoopCache.

To overcome the sequential bottle-neck of DtV, recent works such as WaveFront Decoding (WFD) [19] and LoopSpec [10] introduce wave-style pipelined self-speculative decoding. Ex-ploiting the shared weights across re-current blocks, these methods batch draft and target tokens at different re-current depths into a single global step, as shown in Fig. 2(c). This wave-style pipeline enables early verification and immediate trajectory correction com-pared to DtV, achieving higher token throughput in short-context scenarios. Despite this progress, existing wave-style methods face two critical chal-lenges. First, intermediate loops are underused. WFD and LoopSpec draft only at fixed loops, yet in a looped trans-former every loop can produce a draft token. The predicted next token may change between an early loop and an in-termediate loop. This change is ignored until the final loop verifies the token, and all later tokens built on the outdated draft are wasted. Second, wave-style batching inflates KV cache traffic. The batched tokens in one step sit at different loops, and each token reads the KV cache of its own loop. As Fig. 2(c) shows, each step reads up to R× the KV cache of AR and DtV. In long-context scenarios, this R× memory traffic amplification completely offsets the benefits of pipelined early verification. To address these challenges, we propose LoopCache, an efficient self-speculative decoding framework for long-context looped transformers. First, we observe that token predictions frequently change across recurrent loops, while predictions at later loops increasingly agree with the final token. Based on this insight, LoopCache introduces confidence-guided dynamic draft updating, which monitors intermediate loop predictions and dynamically updates draft tokens. Second, to eliminate redundant KV-cache accesses, we propose a cyclic loop KV cache scheduling strategy. Across global steps, LoopCache accesses the loop-specific KV caches in a fixed cycle (KV R 1 to KV R R), keeping the per-step KV cache access consistent with AR and DtV. To ensure lossless verification, tokens drafted under mismatched KV caches are batched at the nearest upcoming cycle with the matching loop KV cache to compute their exact target loops in parallel. Furthermore, we identify a distinct layer-wise sensitivity across recurrent loops: shallow layers exhibit much larger KV cache variations across loops than deeper layers. By selectively retaining independent KV caches only for a small number of shallow layers during specific steps, LoopCache further improves draft acceptance with negligible memory overhead. 2Our main contributions can be summarized as follows: • We analyze token switch behavior across recurrent loops and propose a confidence-guided dynamic draft updating mechanism. By flexibly leveraging predictions from all inter-mediate recurrent steps, LoopCache dynamically refines draft tokens in flight to avoid wasted computation on stale tokens. • Targeting long-context efficiency, we propose a cyclic loop KV-cache scheduling strategy which keeps per-step KV traffic consistent with AR and DtV, while batching exact verification at the nearest matching KV cycle. We further exploit layer-wise KV differences across loops by selectively decoupling shallow-layer KV caches to improve acceptance rates. • Extensive experiments across multiple Looped Transformers and long-context bench-marks demonstrate that LoopCache achieves average 10.25 × and 14.56 × speedup over WFD and LoopSpec, while delivering average 5.12 × and 2.52 × speedup compared to standard AR and DtV decoding. 

2 Background 

Looped Transformers Looped Transformers extend recurrent computation to standard Transformer blocks by repeatedly applying a weight-shared block for R steps [12, 18, 28, 30, 37, 40]. As illustrated in Fig. 1, current architectures mainly follow two designs: Ouro [40] repeats the entire Transformer stack, whereas Huginn [18] and Raven [28] iterate an internal recurrent block between a non-recurrent prelude and coda. Despite sharing model parameters across all recurrent steps, each loop 

r ∈ { 1, . . . , R } maintains an independent KV cache KV R r because its input hidden states evolve across loops [18, 40]. 

Self-Speculative Decoding Speculative decoding accelerates AR generation by using a fast draft model to propose candidate tokens and a target model to verify them in parallel, where rejection sampling guarantees an identical output distribution to the target model [25]. Self-speculative de-coding eliminates the separate draft model by reusing intermediate computations of the target model itself [5, 13, 32, 34, 38, 39]. In Looped Transformers, early loops naturally serve as draft models for the full-depth model [18, 40]. As shown in Fig. 2(b) and Fig. 2(c), while DtV serializes shallow drafting and deep verification, WFD [19] and LoopSpec [10] overlap these phases via pipelined execution, batching tokens at different recurrent depths into a single step. Specifically, WFD drafts at a predefined depth and organizes in-flight tokens into a diagonal wavefront. LoopSpec further intro-duces a primary proposal at depth d1 and a gated residual proposal at a deeper depth d2. However, this second proposal creates fallback branches that severely inflate the runtime batch size, reaching peak effective batch sizes of 176 and 191 on its two Raven models [10]. Moreover, both methods remain restricted to fixed drafting depths and access multiple loop-specific KV caches within each step. 

Cross-Loop KV Sharing To reduce KV cache overhead, several works share or reuse KV states across recurrent steps. Huginn explores a bounded cyclic cache that overwrites cache slots across loops [18], while Ouro [40] and WFD [19] reuse the last-loop or averaged KV cache during de-coding. Similarly, Mixture-of-Recursions (MoR) reuses the first-loop KV states across subsequent recursions [1]. Although these lossy approaches reduce memory footprint and KV traffic, they inevitably cause accuracy degradation. Specifically, KV states in later loops are iteratively refined, so reusing unrefined first-loop KV states severely compromises model quality. Conversely, reusing the last-loop KV cache is incompatible with standard training and prefill phases, where all tokens are processed in parallel and future-loop KV states are not yet available when computing early loops. This discrepancy prevents effective training adaptation and similarly degrades inference accuracy. In contrast to lossy KV sharing, LoopCache reduces long-context KV traffic while preserving exact loop-specific verification for lossless acceleration.

As illustrated in Fig. 3(a), a draft token may change at an intermediate loop, while subsequent speculative tokens continue along the outdated trajectory. Fig. 3(b) shows that such prediction switches occur frequently, with distinct patterns across models and recurrent depths. This variation highlights the limitations of fixed-depth drafting. Although predictions from every loop could provide additional drafts, retaining all candidate branches can increase the batch size to 2R−1 in the worst case. Such exponential growth is impractical for models with many recurrent loops. Importantly, Fig. 3(c) shows that agreement with the final prediction generally increases with loop depth, indicating that later predictions are more likely to match the final token. This motivates dynamically replacing outdated drafts rather than retaining all candidate branches. As shown on the right side of Fig. 3(a), intermediate predictions can be used to update in-flight drafts and prune their outdated speculative continuations. By guiding these updates with prediction confidence, LoopCache exploits intermediate-loop information while maintaining a single active draft trajectory to avoid excessive branching. 4Observation 2: KV cache traffic limits wave-style decoding in long contexts. WFD and LoopSpec reduce sequential execution by batching tokens at different recurrent depths, but each global step accesses multiple loop-specific KV caches [10, 19]. As shown in Fig. 4(a), despite requiring fewer global steps than AR and DtV, both methods incur substantially higher total KV cache reads. This overhead becomes increasingly critical as context length grows, since decoding becomes dominated by KV cache memory traffic. Consequently, Fig. 4(b) shows that WFD and LoopSpec suffer declining speedups as context length increases, often becoming slower than conventional AR decoding. Directly sharing KV caches between draft and target loops cannot resolve this issue without compro-mising exact computation. When a draft token is accepted, its recurrent computation must remain consistent with the target model. However, using a mismatched loop KV cache during drafting produces incorrect hidden states and KV entries. These states cannot be reused for subsequent target computation without recomputation, even if the draft token itself is accepted. Therefore, lossless acceleration requires reducing KV traffic while preserving exact loop-specific computation during verification. This motivates a cyclic KV cache access strategy that batches exact verification at the corresponding loop cycles. 

Observation 3: Cross-loop KV similarity exhibits strong layer-wise differences. Although exact verification requires loop-specific KV states, the shared weights and iterative refinement of Looped Transformers suggest potential similarities across recurrent loops. Fig. 5(a) shows that the key and value states of corresponding layers exhibit high cosine similarity across loops. This suggests that reusing KV caches from other loops during drafting may preserve useful in-termediate predictions while reducing memory traffic. Combined with exact loop-specific verifi-cation, such approximate reuse offers an opportunity to improve speculative decoding efficiency without compromising correctness. However, cross-loop KV similarity varies considerably across layers. As shown in Fig. 5(b), shallow layers exhibit larger KV differences between consecutive loops, while deeper layers remain relatively stable. Thus, uniformly sharing KV caches across all layers would degrade draft quality and reduce acceptance rates. Instead, selectively maintaining independent KV caches for a few sensitive shallow layers can better preserve intermediate predictions with small memory overhead. This provides an opportunity to improve draft acceptance while retaining the efficiency of cross-loop KV reuse. 

4 LoopCache 

Building on the observations in Section 3, we propose LoopCache, a self-speculative decoding framework that combines dynamic draft refinement with efficient KV cache scheduling. As il-lustrated in Fig. 6, LoopCache uses confidence-guided updates to maintain a single active draft trajectory and cyclically schedules loop-specific KV caches to reduce long-context memory traffic. A separate verification path preserves exact target computation, while selective shallow-layer KV accesses further improve draft quality.

4.2 Cyclic Loop KV Cache Scheduling 

Wave-style decoding overlaps drafting and verification but requires concurrent access to multiple loop-specific KV caches, causing substantial memory traffic in long contexts [10, 19]. To retain pipelined execution without this overhead, LoopCache exploits cross-loop KV similarity for speculative computation while preserving exact loop-specific KV accesses for verification. 

Cyclic KV Scheduling. Instead of accessing different loop KV caches within the same global step, LoopCache fixes the accessed cache according to a cyclic schedule. For global decoding step g ≥ 1,the selected KV loop ID is 

c(g) = 1 + (( g − 1) mod R), (3) where R is the total recurrence depth. Thus, the global steps repeatedly access KV R 1 → KV R 2 →· · · → KV R R. All active token states in each step share the same long-prefix KV cache KV R c(g),reducing the number of distinct full-prefix KV accesses from up to R to one per step. To support this schedule while maintaining exact decoding, LoopCache organizes token computation into two concurrent paths, as shown in Fig. 6(a). 

Spec Branch. The speculative path advances in-flight tokens through their respective recurrent loops while using the KV cache selected by the current global step. Let d denote a token’s current recurrent loop ID. Even when d̸ = c(g), the token uses KV R c(g) to compute its intermediate state and predict subsequent tokens. Although this computation is approximate, the high similarity of KV 6states across loops allows it to provide useful draft predictions. The Spec Branch therefore retains the early drafting capability of wave-style decoding while sharing long-prefix KV accesses across tokens. 

KV Branch. To ensure exact verification, each surviving speculative token is recomputed through the correct loop-specific KV caches. The KV Branch starts at the nearest upcoming complete KV cycle and advances through loops 1 to R in order, using the corresponding KV cache at each loop. This recomputation restores the exact hidden states and KV entries under the candidate prefix, even when the Spec Branch predicts the correct token. The KV Branch provides exact target logits for greedy decoding and rejection sampling. If a candidate is rejected, its dependent speculative states are discarded, and decoding resumes from the corrected token. Intermediate predictions from both branches can trigger confidence-guided draft updates, while the KV Branch determines the committed output. When the two branches have matching recurrent depths, their computations are identical and can be merged into a single path, denoted as Aligned in Fig. 6(a). This avoids redundant computation while preserving exact target verification. 

4.3 Selective Shallow-Layer KV Decoupling 

Although cyclic KV sharing reduces memory traffic, using a mismatched loop cache can degrade draft predictions, especially when the draft loop differs substantially from the current KV loop. As observed in Fig. 5(b), shallow layers exhibit larger cross-loop KV differences than deeper layers. To address this imbalance, LoopCache selectively restores loop-specific KV accesses for a few shallow layers in the Spec Branch. 5.3 Ablation Study 

Fig. 12 evaluates the key design choices in LoopCache. 

Component Contributions Fig. 12(a) shows the cumulative speedup gains of the three components, normalized to the fixed-proposal baseline WFD. M1 denotes confidence-guided draft updating, M2 denotes cyclic loop KV scheduling, and M3 denotes selective shallow-layer KV decoupling. Each component contributes additional gains, with the complete design achieving the best performance. 

Confidence Threshold Fig. 12(b) evaluates the impact of the confidence threshold τ . Pruned states per token measures the number of live speculative token states invalidated by draft replacement or verification rejection, normalized by the number of committed decoding tokens. We select τ = 0 .125 ,which provides the best overall trade-off between speedup and speculative state pruning. 

Shallow-Layer KV Decoupling Fig. 12(c) evaluates different numbers of decoupled shallow layers in terms of speedup, TPF, and time per forward, which denotes the latency of one loop forward. All metrics are normalized to the setting without shallow-layer decoupling. Selecting the first two physical layers as shallow layers preserves high TPF while limiting additional KV accesses and forward latency, achieving the best overall speedup. Accordingly, we set S = {1, 2} and the loop-ID difference threshold to δ = 1 .

6 Conclusion 

We presented LoopCache, an efficient self-speculative decoding framework for long-context Looped Transformers. By exploiting intermediate-loop predictions, confidence-guided dynamic draft up-dating corrects outdated drafts early and avoids unnecessary speculative computation. Cyclic loop KV scheduling substantially reduces memory traffic by sharing loop-specific KV accesses while preserving exact target verification. Selective shallow-layer KV decoupling further improves draft acceptance with minimal additional overhead. Extensive experiments demonstrate that LoopCache achieves average speedups of 5.12 × and 2.52 × over AR and DtV, and 10.25 × and 14.56 × over WFD [19] and LoopSpec [10], respectively. These results highlight the effectiveness of LoopCache in enabling fast and lossless long-context decoding for Looped Transformers. 9References
