## ABSTRACT 

Representations of supervised datasets support higher-level machine-learning tasks such as dataset retrieval, and distribution-shift monitoring. However, ex-isting approaches typically rely either on hand-crafted meta-features or on dataset encoders trained specifically for representation learning. We introduce T AB MF, a framework that repurposes pretrained tabular foundation models (TFMs) as encoders of supervised datasets. T AB MF equips a predictive TFM with an architecture-aware dataset-level readout and adapts the model end-to-end using contrastive post-training on stochastic views of synthetic supervised datasets. We instantiate T AB MF on TabICLv2 and TabPFN-2.5 and evaluate the result-ing representations across six tasks on unseen real datasets. Simple frozen TFM readouts yield competitive representations, indicating that predictive pretraining already induces substantial dataset-level structure. The two T AB MF encoders ob-tain the strongest aggregate performance among the evaluated learned and clas-sical dataset representations, with particularly strong results in dataset retrieval, compact benchmark construction, and distribution-shift severity assessment. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Many machine-learning problems require reasoning about an entire dataset rather than individual observations. A useful representation of a supervised dataset can support dataset retrieval, bench-mark construction, distribution-shift monitoring, transfer of model or hyperparameter knowledge, and identification of rare or atypical datasets. Ideally, such a representation should map a previ-ously unseen dataset to a fixed-dimensional vector whose geometry can be reused across different dataset-level tasks. Existing approaches largely rely on hand-crafted meta-features or dedicated dataset encoders. Meta-features summarize a dataset through a predefined collection of statistics (Alcobac ¸a et al., 2020) and underpin algorithm selection (McElfresh et al., 2023; Zhao et al., 2021) and zero-shot Au-toML ( ¨ Ozt¨ urk et al., 2022; Pineda-Arango et al., 2024), but their representation is fixed by design and cannot adapt to new notions of dataset similarity. Learned alternatives such as Dataset2Vec (Jo-maa et al., 2021) and the Neural Statistician (Edwards & Storkey, 2017) learn dedicated dataset encoders from scratch on collections of datasets, without leveraging the representations acquired by large-scale predictive pretraining. This raises a more general question: can the representations learned through large-scale predictive pretraining be repurposed into reusable representations of entire supervised datasets? We answer this question with T AB MF, a foundation model for supervised dataset representation. Our key idea is to repurpose pretrained tabular foundation models (TFMs) such as TabPFN (Holl-mann et al., 2025; Grinsztajn et al., 2025) and TabICL (Qu et al., 2025; 2026). These models already process an entire labeled table jointly and learn rich internal representations through predictive pre-training, but are optimized for instance-level prediction rather than for representing the supervised dataset itself. T AB MF equips a pretrained TFM with an architecture-aware dataset-level readout and adapts the entire model end-to-end with a contrastive objective over stochastic views of synthetic supervised datasets. ## 2 RELATED WORK 

Meta-features and learned dataset encoders. Hand-crafted meta-features summarize a dataset with fixed statistics (Alcobac ¸a et al., 2020) and underpin algorithm selection (McElfresh et al., 2023), outlier-model selection (Zhao et al., 2021), and zero-shot AutoML ( ¨ Ozt¨ urk et al., 2022; Pineda-Arango et al., 2024). As fixed descriptors, they cannot adapt their representation to the task consuming it, and Herre et al. (2026) report limited ability to explain performance differences between modern tabular foundation models. Some meta-feature families also use explicit target statistics or fit predictive models to the dataset, which can overlap with downstream properties de-fined through predictive behavior; we control this explicitly for epiplexity prediction (Section B.4.4). Learned alternatives train dataset encoders from scratch: Dataset2Vec (Jomaa et al., 2021) distin-guishes batches of the same versus different real datasets with a hierarchical set network, and the Neural Statistician (Edwards & Storkey, 2017) learns a variational summary of a set of exchangeable observations. T AB MF differs in starting from a pretrained TFM rather than a randomly initialized set network, and in using synthetic supervised datasets for dataset-level post-training; both baselines are included in our primary comparison (Section 5). Task2Vec (Achille et al., 2019) embeds tasks via Fisher information of a probe network, which requires fitting the probe on every new dataset; our encoders require only a forward pass. Kunz et al. (2026) align numeric tables through their statistics, without learning and without supervised-task structure. 

Tabular foundation models and their representations. TabPFN (Hollmann et al., 2025; Grinsz-tajn et al., 2025) and TabICL (Qu et al., 2025; 2026) are Transformers pretrained on synthetic data for in-context label prediction; TabDPT (Ma et al., 2025) trains on real data, and CARTE (Kim et al., 2024) incorporates column-name semantics. Although these models are designed for predic-tion, their internal representations have also been reused: Ye et al. (2025) study frozen TabPFN v2 row-level features, while Regenwetter et al. (2026) pool TabPFN-2.5 activations into dataset descrip-tors for curating synthetic pretraining tasks. We repurpose TabICLv2 and TabPFN-2.5 as encoders of supervised datasets using architecture-aware readouts and contrastive dataset-level post-training (Section 3.2), and separately evaluate what their frozen readouts already capture (Section 5.5). 

Contrastive learning and table embeddings. Contrastive methods on tabular data corrupt fea-tures (Bahri et al., 2022) or subset them (Ucar et al., 2021) to construct positive views of individual rows, following the instance-level augmentation paradigm of SimCLR (Chen et al., 2020). T AB MF instead applies contrastive learning at the dataset level: positive pairs are stochastic views of the same supervised dataset that can differ in their sampled observations, observed variables, and label-preserving feature transformations (Section 3.1). A separate line of work learns table representations for content-based retrieval and data discovery, including HyTrel (Chen et al., 2023), the TEmBed benchmark (Vogel et al., 2026; Poostforoushan et al., 2026), and generalist tabular embeddings (Qiang et al., 2026). This setting is distinct from ours: T AB MF represents a supervised dataset (X, y ), with the designated target forming part of the represented object, rather than a target-agnostic table. We nevertheless compare against HyTrel and report TEmBed results separately in Appendix B.7 to examine how supervised dataset representa-tions transfer to content-oriented table tasks. 

## 3 TAB MF 

Let D = ( X, y) denote a supervised classification dataset with T observations, H features, and C

classes, where X ∈ RT ×H and y ∈ { 0, . . . , C − 1}T . We seek an encoder fθ that maps a previously unseen supervised dataset to a fixed-dimensional representation z = fθ (D) ∈ Rdz . The designated target y is part of the represented object, so the embedding is intended to capture predictive structure between features and target rather than target-agnostic table content. TAB MF repurposes a pretrained predictive tabular foundation model (TFM) as such a dataset en-coder. The general recipe combines three components: (i) a pretrained TFM backbone that contex-tualizes an entire labeled dataset, (ii) an architecture-aware readout that reduces its internal states to a single dataset representation, and (iii) contrastive post-training over stochastic views of synthetic supervised datasets. The complete encoder is fully differentiable, allowing the backbone, readout, and projection network to be optimized jointly. At inference time, a dataset is mapped directly to a fixed-dimensional embedding in a single forward pass. 3Preprint: under review We instantiate this recipe on TabICLv2 (Qu et al., 2026) and TabPFN-2.5 (Grinsztajn et al., 2025). We refer to the resulting models as T AB MF-ICL and T AB MF-PFN, respectively, for brevity. The two models organize their internal representations differently: TabICLv2 reduces observations to fixed-dimensional row states before applying cross-row in-context attention, whereas TabPFN-2.5 maintains contextual representations jointly over observations and feature groups. We therefore use a different dataset-level readout for each backbone (Section 3.2). We also evaluate dataset-level readouts of the frozen backbones to measure what predictive pretrain-ing provides before contrastive post-training; implementation details are given in Appendix A.1. 3.1 CONTRASTIVE DATASET -L EVEL ADAPTATION

## 4 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

4.1 TRAINING AND MODELS 

Training. We generate synthetic datasets on the fly using the TabICLv2 Graph-SCM prior (Qu et al., 2026), with 5,000–50,000 rows, 5–100 features, and at most ten classes, retaining the remaining prior settings. Each dataset yields two stochastic views as described in Section 3.1, containing 500– 5,000 rows and 5–50 features for T AB MF-ICL, or 200–1,500 rows and 5–30 features for T AB MF-PFN, capped at the source dimensions. The smaller T AB MF-PFN views reflect its higher memory requirements. Training views additionally apply noise and Fourier transformations to 30% of the selected columns, with noise standard deviation 0.1 and 16 Fourier bins. We jointly optimize the backbone, architecture-aware readout, and projection network using AdamW with a global batch size of 64 datasets, a learning rate of 10 −5, zero weight decay, and a cosine learning-rate schedule with 5% warmup. The evaluated T AB MF-ICL and T AB MF-PFN checkpoints use 45,000 optimization steps. Training uses two NVIDIA H100 GPUs per encoder and requires approximately 315 GPU-hours for T AB MF-ICL and 273 GPU-hours for T AB MF-PFN. 

Encoders and baselines. We evaluate the summary-token and column-aware encoders defined in Section 3.2, instantiated with TabICLv2 (Qu et al., 2026) and TabPFN-2.5 (Grinsztajn et al., 2025). TabICL ICL-mean and TabPFN mean-pooling provide frozen-backbone baselines; comparisons with the adapted encoders therefore include changes in readout. We also evaluate alternative TabICL readouts and random initialization, alongside PyMFE (Alcobac ¸a et al., 2020), Dataset2Vec (Jomaa et al., 2021), the Neural Statistician (Edwards & Storkey, 2017), and HyTrel (Chen et al., 2023). 4.2 EVALUATION TASKS 

We evaluate transfer from synthetic post-training datasets to previously unseen real datasets across the following dataset-level tasks. 

Bootstrap view discrimination. On 191 TabBench datasets (Neuralk-AI, 2025), we test whether views of the same dataset have higher cosine similarity than views of different datasets. Four views are generated per dataset by independently resampling rows and subsampling features, with 500– 5,000 rows and 5–50 features, capped at the source dimensions. ROC-AUC measures discrimination between same-dataset and cross-dataset view pairs. Per-dataset hard margins additionally measure separation from the closest competing views. 

Dataset-property prediction. We probe embeddings for two properties on TabBench. Domain discrimination predicts eight manually assigned domains with a multiclass logistic-regression probe and reports macro one-vs-rest ROC-AUC. Epiplexity measures the structural information that can be extracted from a dataset by a learner of fixed computational capacity (Finzi et al., 2026). For epiplexity prediction, we use prequential estimates obtained with an online logistic classifier as reference values. We train a ridge regressor to predict these estimates from dataset embeddings and evaluate Spearman correlation on held-out datasets. PyMFE uses a leakage-controlled feature set for epiplexity (Appendix B.4.4). Appendix B.4 specifies preprocessing, coverage, and paired analyses. The epiplexity estimator and additional probes are described in Appendix B.4.3. 

Retrieval under transformations. A transformed dataset retrieves its source from a gallery of original datasets by cosine similarity; encoder-specific coverage is detailed in Appendix B.2. The 19 transformations cover row and feature subsampling, recoding, rescaling, missingness, noise, and quantization. Compact benchmark selection. Following Gusev & Zaytsev (2026), we select three datasets and measure how closely their classifier ranking matches that of the full benchmark. The metric is mean Spearman correlation with the full-population classifier ranking (Appendix B.3). 

Distribution-shift severity. On nine TableShift tasks (Gardner et al., 2023), we test whether ID– OOD embedding distances order shifts consistently with their published mean accuracy drops across estimators. Encoders receive the same label-stratified samples of at most 8,192 rows from each official test split, and preprocessing is fitted on the training split. We compute Spearman correlation between cosine distance and the mean ID-minus-OOD accuracy gap, with one observation per task; the nine tasks, severity targets, and sampled population sizes are reported in Table 16. 

## 5 RESULTS 

We evaluate whether T AB MF provides a reusable representation of previously unseen supervised datasets across several forms of dataset-level reasoning. We first summarize performance across the six primary evaluation tasks, then examine robustness to alternative views of the same dataset and applications of the learned geometry to compact benchmark construction and distribution-shift assessment. Finally, we analyze how much dataset-level structure is already present in frozen pre-dictive TFMs and examine the effect of encoder design choices. We select one variant per backbone using bootstrap-view discrimination on 50 synthetic datasets generated with the TabICLv2 Graph-SCM prior (Qu et al., 2026). The selected summary-token and column-aware encoders are used throughout; Appendix B.1 gives the candidate comparison. This synthetic benchmark is used only for variant selection and does not enter the primary evaluation or aggregate ranking. Table 1 summarizes the six primary evaluations on real datasets. 5.1 TAB MF P ROVIDES STRONG DATASET REPRESENTATIONS 

Across the six primary evaluation tasks, the two T AB MF variants obtain the two strongest aggregate ranks, with median ranks of 1.5 for T AB MF-PFN and 2.0 for T AB MF-ICL (Table 1). A T AB MF encoder is the numerical leader on five of the six individual tasks, spanning real-data view discrim-ination, domain prediction, robust retrieval, compact benchmark selection, and distribution-shift severity. The exception is epiplexity prediction, where frozen TFM representations are stronger under the primary linear probe; we return to this result in Section 5.5. These results indicate that the same learned representation can support multiple dataset-level ob-jectives rather than being useful only for the contrastive task used during training. We next examine the strongest effects in more detail. 5.2 TAB MF L EARNS A ROBUST GEOMETRY FOR DATASET COMPARISON 

Both T AB MF encoders transfer the separation learned from synthetic training views to unseen real datasets. On real-data bootstrap-view discrimination, T AB MF-ICL and T AB MF-PFN achieve ROC-AUCs of 0.9981 and 0.9985 , respectively (Table 1). Because several baselines also obtain high AUC, we additionally evaluate the harder per-dataset margin criterion. Both T AB MF variants obtain significantly larger hard margins than all nine baselines in this comparison (Table 4); the margin construction and paired tests are detailed in Appendix B.1. The advantage persists under substantially broader transformations. When recall@5 is averaged across applicable transformations within each dataset, both T AB MF variants significantly outper-form TabICL ICL-mean, while T AB MF-PFN additionally improves significantly over PyMFE (Ap-pendix B.2). The clearest shared advantage occurs under severe feature removal: when only 25% of features remain, recall@5 is 0.884 and 0.937 for T AB MF-ICL and T AB MF-PFN, compared with 

0.653 for TabICL ICL-mean and 0.728 for PyMFE. The two variants show different robustness profiles (Figure 2). T AB MF-PFN better preserves dataset identity under monotone transformations and standardization, whereas T AB MF-ICL is more robust to heavy noise and missingness. Quantization remains a shared limitation, with both variants falling below PyMFE. Overall, T AB MF substantially improves robustness to alternative views of the same dataset, particularly when much of the original feature set is unavailable. 5.3 COMPACT BENCHMARK SELECTION PRESERVES CLASSIFIER RANKINGS 

We next test whether the learned geometry can support a dataset-level decision task that is not used during training. At a three-dataset budget, T AB MF-PFN achieves the strongest classifier-ranking correlation on TabRepo, reaching 0.929 with K-means selection compared with 0.850 for frozen TabPFN mean-pooling and 0.777 for random selection (Table 1). The result is also consistent across the two evaluated selection rules at the three-dataset budget. Under cosine farthest-first selection, T AB MF-PFN reaches 0.823, compared with 0.673 for frozen TabPFN mean-pooling and 0.777 for random selection. T AB MF-ICL also exceeds random selection under both selectors, reaching 0.854 with K-means and 0.815 with farthest-first. The result is robust 7Preprint: under review across nearby compact selection budgets: for each k ∈ { 2, 3, 4}, a T AB MF variant obtains the highest numerical score under both K-means and farthest-first selection. The relative ordering of TAB MF-ICL and T AB MF-PFN varies with the selection budget, and Appendix B.3 reports the full sensitivity analysis. 5.4 EMBEDDING DISTANCES TRACK DISTRIBUTION -S HIFT SEVERITY 

The learned representation also provides a meaningful notion of distance between related dataset distributions. Across the nine TableShift tasks, the Spearman correlation between ID–OOD em-bedding distance and the published mean accuracy drop is 0.933 for T AB MF-ICL and 0.917 for TAB MF-PFN (Table 1). The strongest baseline, TabICL ICL-mean, obtains 0.850 .This evaluation complements transformation retrieval. Retrieval rewards invariance to changes that should preserve dataset identity, whereas TableShift rewards sensitivity to population changes asso-ciated with deteriorating predictive performance. The high observed correlations therefore indicate that the learned geometry can be invariant to nuisance transformations while remaining sensitive to consequential distribution shifts. Because the evaluation contains only nine task-level observations, we do not interpret the numerical ordering as evidence of pairwise superiority between encoders; Appendix B.5 summarizes the statistical scope of the aggregate evaluation. 5.5 PREDICTIVE TFM S ALREADY ENCODE DATASET -L EVEL INFORMATION 

Having established the performance of T AB MF, we ask how much of this dataset-level capabil-ity is already induced by predictive pretraining. The answer is: a substantial part. Without any dataset-level optimization, TabICL ICL-mean achieves a mean retrieval recall@5 of 0.889 , outper-forming Dataset2Vec ( 0.750 ) and the Neural Statistician ( 0.677 ), while remaining close to PyMFE (0.931 ). Frozen TFM representations also encode substantial dataset-property information: TabICL ICL-mean and TabPFN mean-pooling obtain domain AUCs of 0.670 and 0.650 and epiplexity cor-relations of 0.742 and 0.741 , respectively. We use tuned linear probes for the primary property evaluations because they provide a simple, con-trolled measure of how readily each property is accessible from a representation. Under these probes, dataset-level post-training does not uniformly improve property recoverability. Although T AB MF-ICL obtains the highest observed domain AUC ( 0.702 ), the paired analysis does not establish an advantage over the corresponding frozen readouts (Appendix B.4.2). For continuous epiplexity pre-diction, both frozen encoders obtain higher correlations than their T AB MF counterparts, with three of the four cross-comparisons significant after correction (Appendix B.4.3). These primary comparisons characterize linear accessibility rather than total information content. We therefore repeat both evaluations with substantially more expressive nonlinear probes as a ca-pacity check. With a tuned RBF-and-polynomial SVM, T AB MF-ICL obtains a higher domain AUC than frozen TabICL ( 0.700 versus 0.644 ), and the corresponding paired per-dataset log-loss com-parison is significant after Holm correction ( p = 0 .0278 over the full 11-encoder comparison pool). The corresponding PFN comparison remains unresolved (Appendix B.4.2). With a strengthened multi-kernel ridge probe for epiplexity, neither adapted encoder is significantly separated from its corresponding frozen representation under the same full-pool correction scope. The complete epi-plexity results are reported in Appendix B.4.3. Together, these results show that predictive TFM pretraining itself induces useful representations of entire supervised datasets. Dataset-level post-training strongly improves the geometry for robust dataset comparison, but can also change how individual properties are organized in the representa-tion space. The linear results should therefore be read as differences in readily accessible structure; the nonlinear checks show that the apparent ties and losses do not, by themselves, establish that the corresponding information is absent from the adapted representations. 5.6 ABLATIONS

## 6 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

Our findings show that predictive TFMs can support reusable dataset representations across several forms of dataset-level reasoning. We deliberately focus on adapting existing pretrained TFMs with a limited additional training budget. Pretrained initialization outperforms training from scratch for both backbones, supporting this practical reuse strategy. The representations are explicitly super-vised: changing the designated target can change the representation even when the feature table is fixed. Our evaluation covers classification datasets and two pretrained TFM backbones; extending the framework to regression and other architectures would further establish its generality. Post-training uses the TabICLv2 Graph-SCM prior, and the resulting encoders transfer to unseen real datasets. Broader synthetic priors or mixtures of synthetic and real datasets offer a direction for ex-tending this coverage. Different applications may also require different notions of dataset similarity, motivating representations whose geometry can be conditioned on the downstream objective. 

## 7 CONCLUSION 

We introduced T AB MF, a framework for repurposing pretrained tabular foundation models as en-coders of entire supervised datasets. Through architecture-aware dataset readouts and contrastive post-training on synthetic dataset views, T AB MF maps an unseen supervised dataset to a fixed-dimensional representation in a single forward pass. Across six primary evaluations on real datasets, the two T AB MF instantiations obtain the strongest aggregate performance among the evaluated learned and classical dataset representations, with particularly strong results in robust dataset com-parison, compact benchmark construction, and distribution-shift assessment. We additionally find that predictive TFM pretraining alone already induces useful dataset-level rep-resentations, even without explicit dataset-level training. Together, these results show that tabular foundation models can serve not only as predictors over individual observations, but also as a foun-dation for learning reusable representations of supervised datasets. 9Preprint: under review

Populations and views. The real-data population consists of 191 TabBench datasets. Hard margins. For each source dataset, we define the hard margin as the mean within-dataset pairwise cosine similarity minus the maximum cross-dataset cosine similarity involving that dataset. B.4.1 PROBE PROTOCOL 

Encoders receive complete tables and their designated predictive targets when supported; regression targets are quantile-binned into at most ten classes where required by the encoder. Property labels and scores are used only by the downstream probes. We use tuned linear probes for the primary evaluation because they provide a common and interpretable measure of how readily a property can be recovered from each representation. For these probes, robust median/IQR scaling is followed by clipping to ±8 and PCA retaining 99.9% of variance. Preprocessing is refitted within the inner folds of a grid search selecting among 17 logistic-regression penalties or 40 ridge penalties. Classification uses stratified folds and log-loss model selection; regression uses ordinary folds and an SVD ridge solver. The three-fold outer evaluation is repeated five times. PyMFE missing values are median-imputed over the evaluation population before cross-validation; the other checked embeddings contain no missing values. Domain and paired property analyses use a common population of 190 datasets for the main encoder comparisons. Available-case regression coverage is 190 or 191 depending on the encoder, while HyTrel property probes cover 177 datasets. B.4.4 PYMFE C ONTROL FOR EPIPLEXITY
