Abstract
Tabular transformer models such as TabPFN
achieve strong predictive performance but in-
cur substantial inference costs, making them
difficult to deploy in resource-constrained set-
tings. While looped transformers reduce pa-
rameter count through weight sharing, they
can lose significant predictive power when
stopped early.
To address this challenge,
we introduce the Elastically Looped Tabular
Transformer (ElTab), a single model trained
to operate across different computational
budgets through supervision at randomly
sampled early exits.
Across 30 OpenML
classification datasets, a single ElTab model
matches six depth-specific looped models
while cutting training cost by more than half,
and matches a 12-layer TabPFN-style base-
line with 25% fewer inference FLOPs. These
results show that elastic-depth training yields
a single model that adapts to the deployment
budget at hand, offering a favorable trade-off
among predictive performance, storage, and
computational cost.

RELATED WORK
ElTab is related to three lines of research: tabular
foundation models, looped Transformers, and meth-
ods for elastic inference budgets.
Tabular foundation models. Tabular foundation
models are pretrained neural networks that solve new
tabular prediction tasks without task-specific training.
They predict on a new dataset by conditioning the
network on the dataset’s labeled examples alongside
the test queries, producing predictions in a single for-
ward pass. The first such model, TabPFN (Hollmann
et al., 2022, 2025), is pretrained on millions of syn-
thetic datasets sampled from a prior (M¨uller et al.,
2021), yielding strong predictive performance across
diverse tabular benchmarks. Its architecture is based
on the Transformer (Vaswani et al., 2017), with atten-
tion across rows that makes predictions invariant to
row order. However, inference cost grows quadratically
with the number of rows and linearly with the num-
ber of layers. Successive TabPFN versions have grown
deeper (Grinsztajn et al., 2025), raising inference cost
and limiting their use in compute-constrained settings.
One line of work lowers inference cost by replacing
some or all Transformer layers with state-space mod-
els (Koch et al., 2025; Komisarczyk et al., 2026b), trad-
ing some of attention’s expressiveness for efficiency.
This trade-off is fixed by the architecture at training
time. We instead repeatedly apply a few shared layers
and train them elastically, allowing the trade-off be-
tween accuracy and compute to be chosen at inference
time.
Looped Transformers.
Looped Transformers ad-
dress the tension between the need for computational
depth and parameter efficiency by applying the same
layers repeatedly (Giannou et al., 2023). Instead of
learning a deep stack of distinct layers, they learn a
shallow stack and feed its output back as input over
several iterations, refining the representation at no ad-
ditional parameter cost. Applying a single block recur-
rently can match the performance of a deeper stack of
distinct blocks (Dehghani et al., 2018). Small looped
models also match or exceed much larger models on
reasoning benchmarks such as ARC-AGI (Jolicoeur-
Martineau, 2025; Komisarczyk et al., 2026a).
For
tabular data, a single looped nanoTabPFN block
has recently been shown to match a six-layer nano-
TabPFN (Balef et al., 2026). However, looping alone

Elastic inference depth. Deployment settings differ
in the compute they can afford, yet training a separate
model for each budget multiplies training and stor-
age costs. Early-exit networks address this problem
by attaching predictions to intermediate layers, allow-
ing a single model to stop inference before the final
layer (Teerapittayanon et al., 2016; Xin et al., 2020;
Elhoushi et al., 2024). Their shallower exits improve
when trained to match the final prediction (Zhang
et al., 2019). The same principle has been applied to

ditioning each loop on its position and step size allows
truncated models to degrade gracefully (Jeddi et al.,
2026). ElTab extends elastic-depth training to tabular
in-context learning.

TABULAR TRANSFORMERS
Tabular foundation models such as TabPFN are effec-
tive but expensive. Inference compute budgets may
change dynamically, precluding its use. Training sep-
arate models for each budget is infeasible due to high
training and storage costs. We aim to build a single
model that can support tabular predictive inference at
different inference compute budgets. We formalize this
budget-constrained inference as follows,
Given: a labeled context D = {(xi, yi)}n
i=1, unla-

We address this problem by introducing the ElTab
(Elastically Looped Tabular Transformers) family of
models.
ElTab models consist of a small stack of
template layers, applied repeatedly at inference, and
trained to predict after every repetition. The number
of repetitions determines the cost and can be selected
at deployment to fit the budget.

the refined representations to class logits. The tem-
plate stack is applied at most L times, and the de-
ployed model meets a budget by choosing how many

3.2
Training
ElTab is pretrained on synthetic classification tasks
drawn from a prior π, following the training proto-
col of recent TabPFN-style models (Koch et al., 2025;
Komisarczyk et al., 2026b). Each task consists of a
labeled context and a set of query examples. At ev-
ery training step, we sample a shortcut depth k uni-
formly from {1, . . . , L −1} and perform two separate
forward passes through the same shared model: a full-
depth pass with L applications of the block stack and
a shortcut pass with k applications. Both predictions
are supervised using the query labels. In addition, the
shortcut prediction is distilled from the detached full-
depth prediction, which serves as a fixed teacher for
the distillation term. Sampling a new shortcut depth
at each step provides supervision to every depth over
the course of training. We formalize this training ob-
jective below.

an ElTab model with a layer template stack Fθ of B
blocks and a maximum depth of L loops. The model
predicts the labels of queries Xq from a context D, with
N rows in total, and stops after M ∈{1, . . . , L} loops
chosen to fit the inference budget. Denote the cost of
this prediction by C(M, N), the cost of one block on
this input by c(N), and the combined cost of the em-

1. The cost grows linearly with the number of loops:
C(M, N) = c0(N) + BM c(N).

3. For hidden dimension d, the template layer stack
has Θ(Bd2) parameters, independent of M, L,
and N.

Metrics
We assessed each model based on predictive quality
and inference efficiency. Predictive quality was quan-
tified using the area under the receiver operating char-
acteristic curve (AUC-ROC). On each dataset, results
were averaged over five reproducible 50/50 train–test
partitions, with every partition stratified to preserve
all observed classes in both subsets. We present the
mean AUC-ROC in the main text and report its stan-
dard deviation across splits in the appendix.

(Answer, Q1:
Looped vs. TabPFN) To eval-
uate whether looped models can match TabPFN
while spending less compute at inference, we compare
TabPFN with the six fixed-depth looped models and
ElTab on all 30 datasets. Because each loop applies
three blocks and TabPFN applies twelve, four loops
match TabPFN’s compute, and fewer loops use less.
Figure 3 shows how AUC-ROC changes with infer-
ence compute for each model. Each fixed-depth model
comes close to TabPFN at the budget it was trained
for, even below TabPFN’s compute.
For example,
the model trained for three loops matches TabPFN
to within 0.13 points while using 25% fewer FLOPs.
However, each fixed-depth model drops sharply when
run at other budgets, by up to 58.6 points. In contrast,
ElTab stays close to TabPFN across budgets, matching
it to within 0.27 points at the same 25% saving. There-
fore, looped models can match the predictive perfor-
mance of TabPFN at lower inference compute.
(Answer, Q2:
Elastic vs. budget-specific) To
evaluate whether one elastic model can match a set of
budget-specific models, we compare ElTab with the six
fixed-depth looped models, Looped3×1 to Looped3×6.
Since each fixed-depth model commits to a depth at
training time, we consider two ways of choosing that
depth: (1) at random, and (2) an idealized method
with an oracle that knows the inference budget in ad-
vance. Figure 1(a) shows the per-dataset AUC-ROC of
ElTab and both choices at each budget. ElTab tracks
the oracle at every budget, matching it to within 0.12
points on average and 0.45 points at most. In con-
trast, a randomly chosen depth falls well below both,
because a fixed-depth model drops sharply when run
at budgets for which it was not trained (Figure 3).
Figure 4 confirms this pattern on individual datasets.
ElTab matches or outperforms all six fixed-depth mod-
els on 28 of 30 datasets, on average across the six bud-
gets. However, without an oracle, matching ElTab’s
accuracy at every budget would require training all
six fixed-depth models. ElTab instead uses 57% fewer
training FLOPs and 58% less GPU energy (Table 1,
Figure 1(b)), and needs one stored model instead of
six. Therefore, a single ElTab model can match six
fixed-depth looped models across inference budgets,
without knowing the budget at training time.

5
CONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE
WORK
In this work, we addressed the challenge of deploy-
ing tabular foundation models in settings where avail-
able compute is limited and variable. We introduced
ElTab, a looped model trained to predict after every
loop, allowing inference depth to be chosen based on
the budget available at deployment, without requiring
any retraining. One ElTab model matches TabPFN
with 25% less inference compute, and matches a sepa-
rately trained model at every budget for less than half
the training cost. Thus, ElTab’s elastic training en-
ables a single model to adapt to the compute budget
available at deployment.
There are several directions for future work. ElTab’s
elastic training can be extended to adaptive computa-
tion by learning a stopping rule from its per-loop pre-
dictions, spending more compute on difficult queries
and less on easy ones (Bae et al., 2025). It can also be
extended beyond loop counts to computational paths,
by sampling which blocks are applied, skipped, or re-
peated during training (Jeddi et al., 2026). Finally, be-
cause the elastic objective adds no parameters, it can
be extended to larger tabular foundation models by
applying it to their looped backbones. Together with
these directions, elastically trained looped models offer
a favorable trade-off between predictive performance
and inference cost for tabular foundation models de-
ployed under limited and variable compute.
