SLyM: LLM-Driven Symbolic Lyapunov Function Discovery via
Structure Proposal and Coefficient Optimization
Zihao Fu∗, Xingchen Li∗, Keyou You
Abstract— Lyapunov functions are the cornerstone of stabil-
ity analysis for dynamical systems, yet synthesizing a symbolic
form remains difficult as no universal constructive algorithm ex-
ists. To address this challenge, we introduce SLyM, a framework
that harnesses the knowledge and reasoning of large language
models (LLMs) to discover symbolic Lyapunov functions. To
avoid charging the LLM with ill-suited numerical generation,
SLyM decomposes the construction into structure proposal and
coefficient optimization: the LLM draws on its prior knowledge
of Lyapunov constructions to propose the function structure,
whose coefficients then follow from a convex program. Local-
search structures are folded back into supervision, extending
coverage to systems missed by the initial proposer. Across
all benchmarks, SLyM consistently outperforms the evaluated
neural, sum-of-squares, and generative baselines.
I. INTRODUCTION
Lyapunov functions provide the foundational framework
for stability analysis of dynamical systems. For a given
system, once a Lyapunov function is constructed, its stability
can be validated and the result provides key theoretical
support for control design [1], [2], [3]. However, synthesizing
such a function remains notoriously difficult: no universal
constructive algorithm exists, and classical converse theo-
rems guarantee existence without providing any systematic
means to determine a symbolic form [2].
Classical practice relies on parameterized families or
system-specific constructions—such as energies, lineariza-
tions, or backstepping [2], [4]—which are confined to par-
ticular structures and posited anew for each system. Com-
putational tools automate this search over prescribed finite-
dimensional spaces: sum-of-squares (SOS) programming [5]
and fixed-basis methods [6], [7] fix the basis in advance
and optimize only coefficients. Yet fixing the representation
beforehand severely limits expressiveness: even globally
asymptotically stable polynomial systems need not admit any
polynomial Lyapunov function [8], while non-polynomial
dynamics remain largely inaccessible.
Rather than fixing a basis a priori, neural Lyapunov meth-
ods parameterize Lyapunov functions as neural networks [9],
[10]. Despite their expressive flexibility, these models yield
implicit black-box representations whose verification poses
severe scalability bottlenecks—frequently causing satisfia-
bility modulo theories (SMT) timeouts even on modest 4–

To discover such symbolic functions, large language mod-
els (LLMs) offer an expressive, data-driven prior. Pretrained
on extensive mathematical and engineering literature, an
LLM internalizes canonical Lyapunov constructions—from
positive-definite quadratic backbones to energy-like poten-
tials and cross-terms that neutralize nonlinear drift. Further-
more, by exploiting structural cues in the dynamics, the
model can suggest candidate terms tailored to the system,
spanning polynomial and non-polynomial forms alike. The
success of LLMs in mathematical discovery further under-
scores the promise of this direction [11], [12].
We therefore introduce SLyM (Symbolic Lyapunov func-
tions with LLM), an LLM-driven framework that decom-
poses Lyapunov-function construction into two subproblems:
structure proposal, which determines candidate symbolic
terms, and coefficient optimization, which computes their
numerical weights. This separation matters: recent sym-
bolic methods emit complete expressions with embedded
constants [13], [14], burdening generative models with ill-
suited numerical fitting. In contrast, SLyM decouples the
two: conditioned on system dynamics, the LLM leverages its
symbolic prior to propose a coefficient-free structure beyond
rigid fixed bases, while continuous coefficients are obtained
by convex optimization.
To better adapt the LLM to this task, we adopt a two-stage
training strategy. First, supervised fine-tuning on successful
examples teaches the LLM the mapping from system dy-
namics to valid structures. Second, structures discovered by
local structure search are folded back into the training loop,
enabling the model to internalize search-time discoveries and
propose viable structures more directly on unseen systems.
Our contributions are threefold: First, we propose SLyM,
an LLM-driven framework that decomposes symbolic Lya-
punov discovery into structure proposal and coefficient op-
timization, choosing system-dependent structures from a
library of symbolic building blocks while avoiding ill-
suited numerical generation. Second, we develop a search-
augmented training scheme that folds search-recovered struc-
tures back into supervision, extending coverage to systems
missed by the initial proposer. Third, SLyM consistently
outperforms the evaluated neural, sum-of-squares, and gener-
ative baselines across extensive benchmarks. Furthermore, on
systems drawn from the control literature, it finds accepted
candidates different from the reference functions in the cited
publications.

B. Structured Lyapunov Discovery
Definition 1 specifies the conditions a Lyapunov function
must satisfy without prescribing a particular functional form.
A common approach to synthesis is to fix a parametric
family of candidate functions and solve for its unknown
coefficients [6], [7]. In these methods, the structure is chosen
in advance. We follow this structure–coefficient decomposi-

proposal selects a compact set of component functions whose
linear combination forms a candidate Lyapunov function,

numerical weights. An LLM performs the first task, while
a convex program performs the second.

A. Structure Proposal
The first step in the pipeline is to propose a compact
structure adapted to the dynamics, whose form often suggests
suitable terms: nonlinearities in f, such as exponential or
trigonometric factors, tend to call for matching terms in
V . A fixed basis may miss interactions needed by a par-
ticular system, whereas a broad library makes search and
verification expensive. An LLM can use the form of f to
propose relevant symbolic structures; SLyM therefore leaves
coefficient fitting and candidate validation to optimization.
Because the structure controls expressiveness, a missing term
can make (2)–(3) infeasible, while an oversized structure
increases the fitting cost. The proposer samples structures

C. Coefficient Optimization

satisfies the Lyapunov conditions. By (6), the constraints

infinite LP, which we approximate by sampling Ω[6], [7],
[16]. For fixed S, the sampled problem is convex in c; the
combinatorial search is therefore concentrated on selecting
S, while full-domain validation remains a separate step.

IV. EVALUATION ON LITERATURE AND SYNTHETIC
BENCHMARKS
A. Evaluation Setup
Benchmarks. We evaluate on two benchmarks. The lit-
erature benchmark contains 109 published control examples
with declared domains and reference Lyapunov functions;
these systems are held out from both D and P. The synthetic
benchmark contains 600 generated systems, equally divided
among three families from Alfarano et al. [13] and labeled
below: NonPoly is the non-polynomial family of two or
three equations, Poly3 the polynomial family of two or three
equations, and Poly5 the polynomial family of two to five
equations. We follow their data-generation and split protocol,
and exclude any system whose canonical dynamics appear
in a training corpus. The split follows the instance-level
protocol of Alfarano et al.
Baselines. We compare with three neural methods,
ANLC [17], FOSSIL [18], and LYZNET [19]; two SOS
implementations, findlyap [13] and SOSTOOLS [5]; and
the symbolic transformer of Alfarano et al. [13]. We evaluate
eight ANLC configurations and run SOSTOOLS at degrees
2, 4, and 6, validating every feasible solution.
Common protocol. All methods receive the same 300 s
per-system budget on the same machine, spent across their
attempted degrees or configurations as each sees fit. What-
ever candidates a method returns within that budget are
judged by a single validation pipeline for (2)–(3), using in-
terval/SMT checks, dense sampling, numerical falsification,
and an origin-neighborhood routine. Acceptance uses the full
pipeline; counts include formal and numerical screening. A
system is accepted once one of its candidates clears the
pipeline; anything else, a timeout included, is a failure.
SLyM configuration. The proposer is Qwen3.5-2B [20],
sampling K = 50 structures of at most mmax = 10 terms,
with powers up to pmax = 12 and at most 80 operations per
term; local structure search runs from at most eight seeds
over a pool Bf capped at 60 terms. Coefficient optimization
uses γ = 10−2 and ϵ = 0.3 unless the instance declares

V. CONCLUSION
We presented SLyM, which discovers symbolic Lyapunov
functions by decomposing the construction into structure
proposal and coefficient optimization: the LLM proposes a
coefficient-free structure beyond rigid fixed bases, a con-
vex program supplies the weights, and search-recovered
structures folded back into supervision extend the proposer
beyond its training corpus. Across all benchmarks SLyM
outperforms the evaluated neural, sum-of-squares, and gen-
erative baselines, including on the non-polynomial systems
outside the SOS setting. Future work will extend the structure
library and search procedure to more complex systems for
which the current pipeline finds no candidate.
